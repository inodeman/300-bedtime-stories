Piotr peeked through a square window that Papa had cut in the cardboard castle the evening before, edges soft and safe. The castle sat on the rug, a big box hall with a taped tower that Oona had helped start on another day, and now Queisha knelt beside Piotr with a handful of paper fasteners and a curious look. "I want to know how a window can open and close," Piotr said. "And how a flag can spin without falling off. And why the doorway echoes when I hum." Queisha grinned. "Those are good wonders. Let us ask them one at a time, with our hands."

They started with the echo, because it needed no supplies. Piotr hummed a low note inside the hall. The hum came back rounder, as if the box were humming too. Queisha hummed a higher note. It bounced. "The walls are close, so the sound bumps and returns," she said. They tested the idea by opening the front flap. The echo thinned. They closed it. The echo fattened. "Curiosity confirmed," Piotr said, proud of the phrase and also giggling. They did not stay in the box long enough to get too warm. They crawled out, hair a little fuzzy with static, and laughed when a sleeve crackled.

The window was next. Papa had left a second small square of cardboard, the piece that had come out of the hole. Queisha held it over the window. "It covers. It does not swing." Piotr studied the paper fasteners, little metal brads with caps. "If there is one hole, the shutter can swing. Papa, will you make the hole? We will hold the edges." Papa sat close. He made one small hole with his own tool, no rush, the cardboard crunching softly. The children kept their hands on the safe edges only. Queisha pushed the brad through and spread its legs flat on the inside. The shutter pivoted. It was stiff, then, after they wiggled it kindly, it moved. "It opens," Queisha breathed. "It closes." They tried it ten times. On the eleventh it loosened, and they tightened the legs a little. A repair, quick and calm.

The flag question waited on the tower. A paper straw stood up, taped well, with a red paper flag that did not spin. Queisha tilted her head. "If the straw is pinched, it cannot turn. What if a bead sits under the flag as a smooth helper, and the straw goes through the bead?" They found a wooden bead in the craft tin. The straw did not fit. Curiosity did not quit. "What if the flag is on a loose loop of thread around the straw, so the loop can twirl?" Piotr tried. The thread loop was too tight and stuck. He made it a breath looser. Queisha blew. The red flag spun once, twice, and settled. They both stared, delighted. "A loose loop, not a stuck one," Piotr said. "The answer was room to move."

They walked around the castle like explorers who were also the builders. Piotr wondered if a pebble path would sound different from a paper path. They tried both, listening. Pebbles clicked. Paper whispered. "The click path can be for visitors who want to hear themselves coming," Queisha decided. "So no one is surprised." That was a kind use of a curious test. They added a tiny bell from an old ribbon, tied not too high, so a hand could ring it. The bell's voice was small and sweet, a single ting. Piotr rang it, then let Queisha ring it, sharing the new sound.

Inside, they made a map on the wall with crayon, a square for the window, a swirl for the spinning flag, a line of clicks for the path. Queisha drew a question mark and then, because she now knew, she drew a little hinge beside it. "We do not have to leave every question as a question," she said. "We can leave the answer next to it, in case we forget." Piotr added the words loose loop in careful letters. The crayon smelled waxy and familiar. Their knees were pink from kneeling. The castle smelled like cardboard and a bit like the bell's ribbon, dusty and sweet.

They showed Papa the shutter, the spin, and the echo, not as a boast, but as a report of what they had found. Papa tried the shutter and nodded. "You asked, you tested, you changed one thing at a time. That is how makers learn." Piotr felt the praise land softly. He did not add that they were probably the smartest in the street. He said, "The loose loop was Queisha's idea after my tight one failed." Queisha said, "The hinge was Piotr's idea, and you watched so the poke stayed safe." Credit went where it belonged. The castle stood finished, with a window that could close for quiet and open for light.

They rang the bell once more, a gentle ting, and sat outside the click path. Wonder still buzzed, but it was a content buzz, the kind that knows it can return tomorrow with a new question. The fastener caught a point of light. The flag rested, ready to spin if a breath asked it to.

The lantern by the window glowed softly through the open shutter and painted a square of gold on the rug inside the castle. Piotr and Queisha watched that square and felt the pleasure of a question answered by hand. The castle was done. Their curiosity was not used up. It had simply found a hinge, a loop, and a place to echo.
