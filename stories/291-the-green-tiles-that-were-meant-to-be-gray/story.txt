Ilaria lined up six small clay tiles on the studio board. Each tile was shaped like a tiny bridge, with an arch a thumb could pass under. They were tests for the water-gray glaze that would someday cover a painted ferry's rail on the community mural plan. Paper flags labeled the cups: water-gray on the left, leaf-green on the right. The flags had stuck together when the sponge passed. Ilaria, humming, dipped the bridges into the left cup. She liked the way glaze shone like wet stone. She set the tiles to dry and did not look twice.

When the kiln door, already cooled and safe, was opened by the potter, the bridges came out the color of new orchard leaves. Ilaria's stomach made a small dip. Green was beautiful. Green was not the plan. She glanced at the jar shelf. There was a space behind the big water jar where a tile could sit unseen. Her hand moved, then stopped. Hiding would make the potter wait for gray bridges that were never coming. "I think I mixed the cups," she said, voice small and clear. "The flags were stuck. I am sorry. These are leaf-green, and they were meant to be water-gray."

The potter held one bridge to the window light. The green was even and glossy, a good glaze that had simply gone to the wrong job. "Thank you for telling me before I promised gray to the mural painter," the potter said. "A hidden mix-up grows. A spoken one can change jobs." Ilaria let her shoulders drop. The truth had not brought a storm. It had brought a next step. "Are they ruined?" she asked. "They are green," said the potter. "The orchard corner of the mural needs leaf shapes. Bridges can be bridges, or, if we look kindly, arches can suggest leaves cupped together. Come see the plan."

They walked to the window. Across the plaza the mural showed a hill beginning, with pencil leaves still waiting. Ilaria held a green bridge beside the drawing. "If I turn it, the arch looks like a leaf fold," she said. "We could press new tiles as real leaves, and still keep these as testers so we know the green works." The potter nodded. "Yes. And we will unstick the flags and write the words bigger. Honesty gave us a test we did not mean to make, and it is a useful one." Ilaria rewrote the labels in careful letters: GREEN LEAF and GRAY WATER. She said each word aloud so her eyes and mouth agreed.

She shaped three new tiles like simple leaves, not hurrying, and glazed them from the cup she read twice. "Gray is for a fresh batch of bridges tomorrow, when this cup is clearly gray," the potter said. Ilaria agreed. She placed the green bridges in a labeled tray, not behind the jar. The tray said what they were. She liked that. A true label was a kind of kindness to the next person, including her future self, who might forget.

At the window she imagined the mural painter's pleasure at a green that had already been tested. "I almost made you look for gray that did not exist," she said. "And then you chose the other way," said the potter. "That choice is the brave part of honesty. The mix-up was a mistake. The hiding would have been a second one." Ilaria nodded. She washed the brush until the water ran clear, green last, then nothing. The studio smelled like rain on stone, the same smell it always had when truth and clay were both present.

She carried the tray to the sill so the color could be seen, not shown off, just not secret. A neighbor child passing with a grown-up pointed up. "Leaves," the child said, though they were bridges. Ilaria laughed. "They decided to help the orchard," she called, gently, not loud enough to startle. The child waved. Ilaria waved back. The tiles shone like small honest promises.

She wrote a note for the mural painter: Green glaze tested. I mixed the cups and told the potter. These samples are true. The note had no excuses piled on top. It felt light in her hand. Tomorrow's gray bridges could be gray because today she had not pretended. She set the note under the tray edge so it would not fly off in a breeze.

Ilaria read the note once more, aloud, to be sure it was kind and clear. "I mixed the cups and told the potter," she said. The words did not sting now. They sounded like a path. Her friend at the studio, passing with clean hands, asked if the green was a mistake. "It was a mix-up," Ilaria said, "and I did not hide it. Now it is a leaf test." She smiled. Honesty had given the color a true name, and a true name was easier to carry than a secret behind a jar.

The lantern by the window glowed softly on the leaf-green bridges, making them look like the orchard after rain. Ilaria read her new labels once more. Telling the truth kindly had turned a mix-up into a useful color, and had kept a hidden problem from growing behind a jar. She left the tiles where anyone could see what they really were.
