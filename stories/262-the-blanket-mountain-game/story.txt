The shared bedroom looked like a happy storm had sat down to rest. Boris and his cousin Chiara had built roads from picture books, a cave from blankets, and a harbor where socks became boats. They had laughed until their cheeks hurt, in the good way. Now the afternoon was tilting toward quiet time, and the floor had no path left for feet. Boris stood in the doorway with a sock in each hand. Chiara held a pillow that kept slipping.

“This is a lot,” Boris said. He felt the feeling clearly. It was overwhelmed, like his thoughts were wearing heavy boots. “If I start, I might still be tidying when the stars come out.” Chiara nodded. She felt the same bigness, and also a small hope that the mess might tidy itself if they looked away. It did not. A book slid off a hill of sweaters and landed with a soft thump. Both cousins sighed at the same time, then smiled because the sighs matched.

Chiara’s eyes brightened. “What if cleanup is a game with a map?” she asked. “The bed can be Blanket Mountain. Every toy is a hiker who needs a guide home. You can be the map keeper. I can be the guide. Then we trade.” Boris looked at the mess again. A game felt lighter than a chore. “Teams need rules,” he said. “Rule one, we do not toss. We place. Rule two, we cheer small steps. Rule three, nobody’s pile is only one person’s problem.” Chiara bumped her elbow gently against his. “Agreed.”

They began at the sock harbor. Boris pointed to the drawer. “Hiker socks, your dock is here,” he announced in a captain voice that was still kind. Chiara walked each pair across the room and folded them so the toes kissed. When a blue sock hid under the bed, they kneeled together and found it beside a marble of dust that made them both sneeze and laugh. Boris marked an invisible check on his pretend map. The overwhelmed feeling took off one heavy boot. There was still a lot to do, but the next step was visible.

The picture books became a train. Chiara lined them by size while Boris drove the train, slowly, onto the shelf. “No speeding on the shelf tracks,” he said, and Chiara answered, “Station master says thank you.” A torn paper corner stuck out of one book. They did not yank it. They smoothed it and slid the book in gently. Teamwork, Boris noticed, was also careful hands. He felt proud of that thought and saved it for later, the way he saved smooth stones.

Then came Blanket Mountain. The blankets were the biggest hikers of all. Together they lifted, shook the crumbs into a bin, and folded. The first fold was crooked. The second fold was better. Chiara held the corners while Boris brought the edges to meet. “Your turn to call the count,” she said. “Fold, fold, pat,” Boris counted. They stacked the blankets at the foot of the bed until the mountain was neat instead of wild. The pillow that had slipped now had a clear place. The floor appeared again, pale and surprising, like a path after snow.

They traded jobs for the last corner, where blocks had nested inside a hat. Chiara became map keeper and Boris became guide. She remembered the rule about cheering. “That green block found its box,” she said. “Good guiding.” Boris felt his face warm in a pleased way. He had worried he was slower than Chiara. The worry loosened when she waited without hurrying him. Waiting kindly was part of being a team. He placed the last block and closed the lid with a soft click. Then Chiara found one last sock hiding in the hat, and instead of groaning they both cheered as if the sock had won a tiny race. They walked it to the drawer together, one hand each, because some hikers liked company.

They stood in the middle of the room and turned in a circle. The cave was folded. The harbor was a drawer. The road of books was a calm shelf. There was space to sit. Chiara flopped onto the rug and patted the spot beside her. “We did it because we shared the map,” she said. Boris sat. “And because we made rules that were kind.” Their overwhelmed feelings had shrunk to the size of ordinary breaths. The room smelled like clean cotton and a little like the orange soap from their hands. Before they sat for long, Boris had an idea that kept the team going. “Let’s make a welcome sign for the next time we play,” he said. They used one paper and two colors. Chiara drew the blanket mountain with a path. Boris drew sock boats lined up like polite guests. Under the picture they wrote, We tidy as a team, then we play again. They taped the sign inside the closet door, not crumpled, not crooked. Making the sign felt like putting a smile on the work. Boris noticed that teamwork did not end when the floor was clear. It could leave a kind plan behind for tomorrow. Chiara added both their names, side by side, the same size.

The lantern by the window glowed softly across the tidy rug. Boris and Chiara leaned shoulder to shoulder and listened to the quiet they had made together. Tomorrow they might build the sock harbor again. Tonight the hikers were home, the mountain was smooth, and teamwork felt as cozy as a folded blanket.
