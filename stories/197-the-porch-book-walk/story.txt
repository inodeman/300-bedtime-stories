Three picture books waited in a soft bag on Wynn's steps, their corners protected by a clean towel. One cover showed a red boat. One showed a garden at night. One showed a goat with a polite hat. Jamila swung the bag gently so the books bumped like quiet footsteps. "Boat back to my house, garden back to Ivo, goat back to you," she said, then paused. "Or is the goat yours and the boat Ivo's?" The afternoon smelled like warm dust and the orange peels someone had set out for the birds. Friendship, they were about to learn, includes checking before you knock.

Wynn opened the bag. The boat book had a crumb of cracker in the gutter of a page, which felt like his snack, not Jamila's. The garden book had a blade of grass pressed flat near the back, a green whisper from Ivo's step. The goat book smelled faintly like Jamila's lemon soap. "We almost gave the goat a boat ride to the wrong door," Wynn said. Jamila laughed, a small embarrassed laugh that asked to be forgiven. "I mixed them when I hugged the bag," she said. "Thank you for looking." They sat down right there, because a sidewalk is a fine office for friends.

They made name cards from torn paper, written big. Wynn drew a tiny boat next to his name. Jamila drew a goat's hat, a little crooked, which made it better. For Ivo they drew a grass blade. They slipped each card under the towel with the right book. The pencil felt short and friendly in Jamila's hand. A neighbor's sprinkler ticked two yards away, and a cool drop reached Wynn's ankle. He did not mind. The mix-up had become a plan. Plans are one of friendship's favorite tools.

They went to Jamila's porch first, not to leave her, but to set the goat book on her own shelf so it would be home and not traveling anymore. Her door was open to the breeze. The lemon smell was stronger inside the doorway. She patted the goat's hat on the cover. "Stay," she told it, as if it might wander off to sea. Then she came back down the steps with empty hands and a fuller smile. Wynn waited, shifting the bag so the remaining books stayed flat. "Two porches to go," he said. "And you are still on the walk. That is the good part."

Ivo's herb pot brushed their legs with a mint smell as they climbed his step. He opened the door before they knocked, because he had seen them from the window and had been hoping. "Garden book," he said, already glad. Wynn showed the grass card. "We almost took it to a boat," he admitted. Ivo waved that away like a gnat, not unkindly. "You brought it to the grass. That is what matters." They sat on the step, three in a row, and read one page only, the page where the garden hears an owl that is really a teapot. Their shoulders touched. The mint tickled. Nobody rushed the sentence.

The last stop was Wynn's own porch again, which felt funny, like a story that circles home. The boat book slid onto his shelf beside a shell. Jamila read the name card aloud as if it were a medal. "Wynn, captain of returns," she said. He bowed with the bag still in his hands, and the towel slid, and they caught it together, laughing. The laugh was better than being perfect. Friendship had room for a hug that mixes books, as long as someone tells the truth and someone else stays to finish the walk.

They made one extra card that said "look inside first" and tucked it into the bag for next time. Ivo, who had walked the last bit with them, added a drawing of three pairs of shoes. The shoes were all different and all going the same way. The orange peels on the rail had dried at the edges and still smelled bright. Wynn shook a cracker crumb out of the boat book onto the step for an ant that might pass later. Jamila tied the towel's corners so the bag would be ready, not slumped.

They did not need more errands. They needed the minute they were already having, shady and ordinary and sweet. The sprinkler stopped. The sidewalk kept their warmth. Three friends, three books in the right rooms, and a page about a teapot owl still funny in their ears. Wynn said, "Come back when the goat wants an outing." Jamila said, "Only if the goat wears its name." Ivo said, "And only if we read at least one page before anyone goes home."

The lantern by the window glowed softly on Wynn's porch as the three of them waved one more time from the sidewalk. The name cards showed pale in the bag's opening, boat, hat, and grass. Friendship had walked the whole short lane and come back fuller than the bag, warm as the step, ready for the next shared page.
