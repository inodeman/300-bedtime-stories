Karim arrived at the shared table with his picnic basket held in both hands, as if the rolls inside might bounce away. The neighborhood bakery had sent him off with more cinnamon rolls than one boy could hug. Chioma came from the other direction with her own basket, the lid tied with a strip of cloth. Inside, a warm jar of soup knocked gently against a bundle of orange peels and a little pot of honey. Reina was already at the table, smoothing a clean cloth and setting out cups that caught the Saturday light.

"I packed the rolls in a spiral so they would not squash," Karim said. He kept the lid closed. "If I open it, the shape might fall apart."

Chioma nodded hard. "I packed the soup so it would not tip. If I open mine, the peels might slip under the jar." She sat down and placed her basket on her knees instead of on the table. The two baskets faced each other like shy neighbors.

Reina looked from one lid to the other. The bread ovens across the way breathed out a toasty smell. A market stall clinked honey jars. The long table had room to spare. "The food is safe," she said. "And there is a lot of it. What if the baskets are worried, but we are not?"

Karim peeked. The rolls were golden, dusted with cinnamon, and not as fragile as he had feared. Chioma untied her cloth. Steam rose from the soup jar, carrot-sweet and threaded with orange. Nothing spilled. A peel curled on the napkin like a smile.

"I thought I would eat only my rolls," Karim said. "I counted them. There are enough for me and still enough to put back."

"I thought I would eat only my soup," Chioma said. "I like knowing where everything sits."

Reina poured water into the cups, patient and splashless. "You can still know where things sit," she said. "We can make a map. Rolls on this plate. Soup in that bowl. Peels in a little dish. Honey in the middle so both foods can borrow it. Opening a basket is not the same as losing it."

They tried. Karim lifted the rolls one at a time and set them in a ring. The spiral shape changed, but the ring was pretty too. Chioma spooned soup into three bowls. The jar was still half full when she stopped. She liked seeing the extra. It meant the table could welcome another spoon if someone wandered over from the stalls.

Karim tore a roll and dipped a corner into the soup. His eyes widened. "The cinnamon meets the orange," he said. "It tastes like the bakery and the market shook hands."

Chioma spread a bead of honey on a warm piece and listened to it sink. "I was saving the honey for later," she said. "Later can be now if we share it." She pushed the pot toward Karim. He pushed the fullest roll toward her. Reina took a bowl and a roll and did not rush either one.

They talked about the packing while they ate. Karim showed how he had lined the basket with a cloth so the rolls would not stick to the wicker. Chioma showed how she had folded a towel around the soup jar so the heat stayed gentle. Reina listened, then said, "Those were careful steps. Careful can include sharing. You did not have to choose."

A breeze moved the orange peels in their dish. One peel showed its bright side, one its soft white side. Chioma turned them like tiny boats. "We could take peels back to the bakery for the next glaze," she said. "And leave two rolls here for anyone who sits down after us."

"Plenty," Karim said, and he meant it. His basket was lighter, but his plate was more interesting. He was not missing anything. He was discovering the soup he would not have tasted with the lid shut.

They wiped the table with slow circles. The bowls were empty because they had wanted seconds, not because there had been too little. The jar still held a quiet cup of soup. The plate still held rolls. Reina set the honey where the sun could find it, a gold light inside glass.

"Same table," she said. "Two baskets. One meal that tastes bigger."

Karim laughed. "My spiral became a ring. I like the ring."

Chioma tied her cloth around the leftover jar so the walk home would be steady. "Next Saturday I will pack extra on purpose," she said. "Extra is a friendly word."

Karim lifted the basket lid all the way and laughed at his old worry. The rolls had kept their softness even after the spiral became a ring. Chioma spooned a little more soup into Reina's bowl because the jar still looked proud and heavy. They named the Saturday sounds they could hear, the clink of honey jars, the tick of bread ovens, and the bright talk from the stalls. Nothing ran out. The peels still curled. The honey still shone. Sharing had not shrunk the meal. It had given it more colors and more reasons to stay.

The lantern by the window glowed softly over the cloth they had shared. Karim, Chioma, and Reina rested their hands on the warm wood. Picnic baskets sat open and unworried underneath the table. Cinnamon, orange, and honey lingered in the air, and the long table looked ready for the next pair of lids to lift without fear.
