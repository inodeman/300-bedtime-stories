Giorgio's loaf was so warm it pinked the air above it. He had just brought it from the neighborhood bakery bread oven to the Saturday market path, and the crust tickled his palms. He shifted the loaf from hand to hand and smiled a helpless smile. The shared table was only a short walk away. The walk felt longer when the bread was a friendly little sun.

Sable saw him from the picnic cloths. She had an extra cloth in her basket, soft, clean, and big enough for a loaf. Emile stood beside her with a honey jar and a dish of orange peels, on his way to the same table. The market was busy in a bright way. Nobody was unkind. Giorgio was simply under-clothed in the hand department.

"Your bread is celebrating," Sable said. "Your hands are asking for a pause."

Giorgio laughed. "I can make it. I just do a little dance every three steps."

"You can make it," Emile agreed. "And you do not have to dance a hot dance. Kindness has a cloth."

Sable held out the extra wrap. She did not toss it and hope. She stepped close and, with Giorgio's nod, folded the cloth around the loaf the way the bakery folded towels around soup jars. The heat stayed inside. The outside of the bundle became holdable. Giorgio's shoulders dropped. "It feels like the bread is wearing a coat," he said.

"Coats are for caring," Sable said. "I brought two cloths because I like choices. I still have one for my own basket. Nothing is emptied."

They walked together. Emile offered to carry the honey so Giorgio could hug the bundle with both arms if he wanted. Giorgio took the offer. The three of them matched their steps. Patience kept them from hurrying through the crowded bit by the spice stall, where cinnamon bark scented the air. A fast bump would have been silly when a slow swerve would do. They swerved. The loaf did not slide. The peels in Emile's dish gave a citrus whisper each time the breeze moved.

At the shared table they unwrapped the loaf as if it were a gift, which it was. Steam rose. The crust crackled. Sable's cloth had kept the warmth without trapping it into sogginess, because she had folded loosely, with a little door for steam. Emile set the honey and the peels beside the board. There were already rolls in a basket and soup in the pots inside, so this loaf was another verse in a long song of enough.

"May I tear the first piece for you?" Giorgio asked Sable. "You loaned the coat."

"Tear it for the middle," Sable said. "Then it is for all of us, and for whoever sits next. That is my favorite kind of thank you."

They did. The middle piece shone with a soft crumb. Honey made a gold path. Orange peel made it bright. Giorgio's hands were comfortable now, free to hold a cup, free to pass the jar. Emile told a small story about a stuck lid from another morning, and how a warm cloth had been kinder than a knock. Sable listened and smoothed her wrap so it could be used again, a crumb shaken off, no fuss.

Giorgio looked at the path they had walked. "I would have arrived," he said. "I might have arrived frowning at my fingers. This way I arrived smiling at you."

"That is a good trade," Emile said. "A cloth for a smile, and the bread still whole."

Sable wrote a line on a recipe card near the honey. If something warm needs a walk, offer a loose cloth. Walk beside, not ahead. Share the middle when you arrive. She did not write her name in big letters. Kindness did not need a trumpet. The card leaned on the jar, readable and quiet.

They saved slices for the stall and still had a handsome heel on the board. Picnic baskets under the table held more. The ovens inside would bake again later. Plenty sat around them like a calm sea. Giorgio flexed his hands and laughed at how ordinary they felt, no longer doing the hot dance.

Cinnamon drifted from the open bakery door. Market voices braided with the clink of jars. The three friends ate without rushing, passing peels and stories in the same easy circle.

Sable shook the cloth once more and folded it into a square that could live in Giorgio's basket for the walk home. The loaf would be cooler then, but the habit of care could stay. Emile set a peel on the heel of bread like a bright button and did not mind when Giorgio moved it to the center plate instead. Center was the better button. They listened to the market and the ovens at the same time, a duet of clinks and ticks. Giorgio flexed his fingers and told the story of the hot dance until they were laughing again. The laugh was easy because nobody's hands hurt and nobody's bread had fallen. Kindness had been a simple offer. It still felt large, sitting there in the folded square, ready for the next warm thing that needed a gentle trip across the path.

The lantern by the window glowed softly on Sable's folded cloth and the warm heel of bread. Giorgio, Emile, and Sable rested their elbows on the shared table. A small coat for a loaf had made the walk kinder, and the kindness was still there, sweet as honey, ready to be offered again.
