The cloakroom smelled of wool, wet leaves, and the orange soap from the sink around the corner. Hooks marched along the wall in a tidy row, some high, some low. Sora hung her own coat on a middle hook and smoothed the sleeve so it would not drag. A breeze from the open door lifted scarves and set them down. Nico stood on his toes beneath a high hook, his new coat bunched in his arms. The loop missed. The coat slid down his front in a soft heap. He picked it up and tried again, careful, not cross, just stuck. The loop was a small cloth tunnel, and the hook was a question he could not quite answer.

Sora did not grab the coat. Grabbing would finish the job and steal the learning. She stepped closer so he would know he had company. "That hook is a sky hook," she said. "There is a friendlier one lower down, with a blue dot. Blue dots are for coats that are still growing into the high ones." Nico looked, found the blue dot, and let his heels touch the floor. "I did not see the dot," he said. "I saw everyone else up high." Sora nodded. "High is not better. High is just high. May I show you the loop trick on the blue one? Your hands stay on the coat." Nico held the shoulders. "Show me."

She touched only the loop, pinching it open so the hole was obvious. "The hook wants this little tunnel, not the whole collar. If the collar goes on, the coat thinks it is a cape and jumps off." Nico smiled at the idea of a jumping cape. He fitted the loop over the blue-dot hook himself. The coat settled, sleeves quiet, hem above the floor. He tugged once, gently, to test. It stayed. "I did it," he said, surprised and pleased in the same breath. "You did," Sora said. "I only opened the tunnel. You found the hook." The cloakroom felt warmer, though the breeze had not stopped. A mitten tried to escape a pocket and Sora tucked it back, a tiny extra kindness on the way.

Nico practiced once more, off and on, so the trick would stick. The second time was smoother. The third time he talked himself through it. "Tunnel, not cape." Sora watched and did not correct the words, because the words worked. Mr. Adeyemi paused in the doorway with a stack of picture books. He saw the blue-dot success and the two calm faces. "That is a good lesson for the whole row," he said. "Sora, would you like to say the tunnel trick at gathering, or keep it as Nico's story to tell?" Sora looked at Nico. "It is your coat. You tell it if you want." Nico stood taller beside the blue dot. "I can tell it."

At gathering he did, in a short speech. "Use the loop. Low hooks are not baby hooks. They are right-size hooks." The class clapped the soft indoor clap. A few children changed their coats to better hooks without any fuss. Sora felt a glow that did not need her name on it. Kindness that left the other person able was the kind she wanted to practice. She sharpened a pencil that was not hers because it was lying blunt in the tin, then put it back sharp. Small helpfulness could be a habit, like hanging a sleeve straight.

At home time the breeze had become a push. Nico's coat came down the right way, loop first, no heap. He slid his arms in and found the mitten where Sora had tucked it. "You stopped my mitten from going on an adventure," he said. "Adventures are better when you choose them." Sora laughed. "Exactly." They walked as far as the school gate. Nico's parent was there, and Nico demonstrated the loop on his parent's finger, which was not a hook but served. Everyone laughed. Sora waved and turned toward her own street, coat snug, the day neatly hung.

She thought about sky hooks and blue dots while she walked. It would have been faster to hang the coat for him. Faster was not always kinder. The longer way had given Nico a skill he could use in winter, when coats were heavier and hooks were just as high. Leaves skittered. The bakery on the corner breathed out a honey smell. Sora bought nothing and still felt rich. She had spent attention, which grew back. Her pocket had a smooth stone from yesterday, and she touched it for luck that was really just memory.

At her door she hung her coat on a hook that was the right height for her, and she noticed, really noticed, that rightness. She set her shoes in a pair. The house smelled like rice and cinnamon. She told the tunnel story at the table, giving Nico the best lines. The people who loved her listening smiled. The story did not grow teeth or trouble. It stayed a small true thing about a loop and a choice.

The lantern by the window glowed softly in the school cloakroom she pictured as she looked at her own evening window, and the sentence of light felt as simple as a blue dot. Sora was glad she had helped without taking over. Somewhere a coat hung safely because a loop had been shown and a child had done the last, most important part himself.
