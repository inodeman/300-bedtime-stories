The Saturday market stall wanted to open, and the jobs arrived all at once. Hana held a stack of honey jar labels. Giorgio had an armful of orange peels that tickled his chin. Laleh clutched the cloth for the shared table and also, somehow, a tin of cinnamon. The neighborhood bakery door stood open beside them, breathing out warm bread. Soup pots inside tapped their lids. Nobody was late. Everybody was tangled.

"I will hang the labels," Hana said.

"I will hang the labels," Giorgio said at the same time, because he could not see the table past the peels.

"I will put the cloth down if my hands become free," Laleh said, and then she laughed, because her hands were a bouquet of tasks.

They bumped in a soft, harmless way. A peel fell onto the cloth. A label stuck to Giorgio's sleeve. The cinnamon tin gave a tiny rattle. It was funny for a moment and confusing for the next moment. Teamwork had not started yet. Only eagerness had.

Hana took one step back. "Let us count," she said. "Counting can be a map. One, jars. Two, peels. Three, cloth. We will say the numbers together so our hands know the road."

They tried. "One," they said, and only Hana touched the jars, setting them in a row where the sun could turn the honey gold. Giorgio and Laleh kept still, which was a kind of work too. "Two," they said, and Giorgio laid the peels in a bright fan. He shook the label off his sleeve and gave it back without fuss. "Three," they said, and Laleh spread the cloth on the shared table, smooth as a calm pond. The cinnamon tin went in the middle, a brown drum waiting for its spoon.

The pattern was so helpful they used it again. One, recipe cards in a basket. Two, picnic baskets tucked underneath so the walkway stayed clear. Three, a plate of bakery buns for anyone who wanted a taste while they browsed. They did not rush the numbers. If someone started early, they giggled and began the count over. Patience made the giggle part of the plan instead of a mess.

"One was lonely when I tried to do one, two, and three," Giorgio said.

"Three was wrinkly when I held it with the peels," Laleh said. "Now the cloth looks like it slept well."

Hana aligned the last jar so its label faced the path. "That is teamwork. Each number gets all of our attention, even if only one pair of hands moves."

A shopper arrived with an empty spot in a basket and a smile. The stall was ready. Honey, peels, cinnamon, and cards each had a home. The shopper chose a jar and a curl of peel for baking later. There was still a full row left. Hana did not worry. The grocery was abundant, and the bakery next door had loaves cooling in a long golden line.

They carried a small plate to the shared table and sat for a bite before the next rush of cheerful errands. The bun was soft. The cinnamon from the tin smelled like the bun's cousin. Laleh placed a peel on her piece. Giorgio poured a careful honey ribbon. They counted one, two, three in whispers just to feel the rhythm in a resting way.

"We could teach the count to the soup ladle," Giorgio said. "One stir, two taste, three share."

"The ladle already knows," Hana said. "We are the ones who forget when we grab."

They wrote the stall song on a card. One jars. Two peels. Three cloth. If we tangle, we laugh and count again. The card went under the cinnamon tin, where the next morning would find it. Market voices rose around them like birds that had learned manners. The ovens hummed. The cloth stayed smooth because nobody dragged a basket across it.

Laleh swung her feet under the bench. Giorgio stacked the spare labels in a tidy brick. Hana looked down the row of jars and felt the morning settle into its proper shape. Eagerness was still there. It just had a song to walk with.

They ran the count once more just for the pleasure of it, slower, with a bow at the end that made Giorgio's peels flutter. One, the jars gleamed. Two, the orange fan looked like a sunrise. Three, the cloth stayed smooth under Laleh's hands. Hana added a fourth beat they had not planned, a shared smile, and they kept it. Four, they said, and nobody had to hold a thing. The smile was the rest note. A shopper returning for a second jar saw the calm row and said the stall looked like a song. Hana answered that it was a counted song, and the shopper left with honey, a peel, and a recipe card that had room to spare. The bakery ovens breathed heat across the path. Soup smelled ready. The tangle of the early minute was only a story now, a funny one, with a neat ending they could tell while they passed the buns.

The lantern by the window glowed softly at the edge of the smooth cloth. Hana, Giorgio, and Laleh rested in the count they had made, honey sweet on their tongues and orange bright in the bowl. The stall stood ready, and the shared table looked like a place where numbers knew how to be kind.
