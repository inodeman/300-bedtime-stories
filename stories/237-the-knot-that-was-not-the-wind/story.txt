Quinlan looked at the twisted kite string and felt his cheeks grow warm. Jovan held the red kite above the grass, ready for the respectful breeze that was already turning the bush leaves. The sky was clear and friendly. The tangle was not the sky's fault. Quinlan had looped the string around the spool the wrong way while he was hurrying to be helpful. The kite had wobbled on the first small rise and slipped back down. A quick excuse hovered in his mouth. He could say the breeze was rude. He could say the kite was crooked. Honesty asked him to pick a truer sentence.

He swallowed the excuse. "Jovan," he said, "I twisted the string. The wobble was my knot, not the wind." Jovan lowered the kite at once and looked at the spool, not at Quinlan's face in a blaming way. "Thank you for saying the true part," Jovan said. "A true part is easier to untie." Quinlan's cheeks cooled. The hill felt kind again. They sat down together and turned the spool slowly until the loop loosened. Nobody sighed a sharp sigh. The breeze waited, polite, moving the grass in small silver turns.

As they worked, Quinlan told the whole small story. He had wanted to be fast. Fast had made a hidden loop. The loop had made the kite nod. Saying each step kept him from hiding inside a fog of almost-truths. Jovan nodded at the steps. "I do that too sometimes," he said. "I once called a tangle a mystery when it was my shoe on the string." Quinlan laughed, surprised and relieved. Honesty from a friend made honesty feel less lonely. The string came free with a soft whisper. It lay in a clean curve, ready to be wound the right way.

They wound it together. Quinlan called out the direction before each turn so he would not invent a new mistake in silence. "Over, then around." Jovan answered, "I see it. That matches." Checking out loud was another kind of truth. When the spool was neat, they did not leap into the launch. Quinlan held the string up. "Is it really clear, or do I only hope it is?" Jovan looked from spool to kite. "It is really clear." Quinlan smiled. He liked the difference between hope and a looked-at fact. Both were good. They were not the same.

The leaves flicked. Jovan lifted the kite. Quinlan let the string slip in a smooth feed. The breeze slid under the cloth and lifted it above the bush with no wobble at all. The tail drew a calm curve. Quinlan felt joy that was clean because it was not standing on a fib. "The respectful breeze was ready the whole time," he said. "My twist was the only muddle." Jovan watched the kite stand in the blue. "And your sentence was the broom." They both grinned at the picture of a sentence with bristles, sweeping a path for a kite.

They traded jobs halfway, because a true team still shares. When Quinlan held the kite, he told Jovan about a tiny fray he spotted on the tail ribbon. It would have been easy to ignore. "There is a small loose thread," he said. "It is not an emergency, but it is real." Jovan thanked him, and they landed the kite gently to tie the thread down before it could grow. The fix took a minute. The next flight was even steadier. Honesty about a little thing had protected the fun. Quinlan stored that lesson beside the bigger one about the twist.

On the grass, after the folding, Quinlan practiced the sentence he wanted to remember. "I will say what I did, and then I will help untie it." Jovan practiced a reply. "I will be glad to hear it, and I will help too." They were not performing. They were making a habit the way you wind a string, loop by loop. The hill was quiet. The bush was still. The harbor below shone. Quinlan felt lighter than he had at the start, as if the tangle had been on his shoulders and the true words had lifted it into the same breeze that took the kite.

They walked down the path with the kite between them. Quinlan did not retell the excuse he had not used. He retold the fix. That seemed like the right kind of story to carry home. A clear sky, a clear string, and a clear sentence. He hoped the next muddle, whenever it came, would meet the same brave ordinary truth.

They tested the repaired tail with one short hop of the kite, no higher than the bush, to be sure the thread would hold. It held. Quinlan announced the result plainly. "The thread is tied, and the string is clear, and I am glad I said both things." Jovan repeated the facts so they would ring true twice. Facts, they noticed, felt as light as the kite when nobody tried to decorate them. The respectful breeze ruffled the ribbon and then let it rest. Quinlan wound a finger's length of spare string into a neat extra loop, a small careful habit to match the honest words he meant to keep.

The lantern by the window glowed softly as Quinlan set the spool beneath it and looked at the neat line of string. Nothing was twisted now. Outside, the evening was calm and blue. He felt proud in a peaceful way, because honesty had untangled the afternoon faster than any made-up story about the wind.
