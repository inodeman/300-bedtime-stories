Ivo held a blank card and a yellow crayon as if both might speak before he did. The short clothesline between the fences clicked its pins in the breeze. Basil stood with two wooden clothespins in his palm, smooth from other days of use. A bowl of lemons sat on the step, bright as small suns, a gift from the neighbor who grew the tree. Ivo's thank-you lived in his chest and did not want to come out of his mouth. The card was another way, if he could be brave enough to hang it where someone would actually see it.

"You do not have to shout," Basil said. "You can write. I will stand with you." The line was low, tied by Papa that morning between two fences, with the knots on the far side of the posts so the children would not fuss with tight loops. It was a kindness line, Papa had said, for cards, not for heavy wet sheets. Ivo wrote slowly. "Thank you for the lemons. They smell like morning." His letters leaned, and he let them lean. Courage did not require perfect slopes. The crayon smelled waxy and warm from his hand.

They hung Basil's card first, a blue swirl that meant the sky had been nice about the lemons too. One pin held the top. The breeze tested it. The card fluttered, lifted, and sailed into the rosemary like a shy bird. Basil blinked. Ivo's heart thumped, partly for the lost card and partly because his own card was still in his hand, waiting. They found the blue swirl caught on a soft stem, unharmed. "One pin is a maybe," Basil said. "Two pins are a promise." He did not sound angry at the wind. The wind was only doing its job too well.

Papa heard the flutter story and brought nothing but a nod. "Two pins, and the card faces the neighbor's gate," he said. "Keep your fingers on the card's edge, not inside the pin's bite." They practiced on a scrap. The pin closed with a neat wooden clap. Ivo liked the sound. It was small and decided. He still felt shy. Shy and brave can share a shirt. He stood under the line until the shyness made room. Basil did not rush him. A lemon in the bowl glowed. Somewhere a bird tried three notes and settled.

"I will hang mine," Ivo said. His voice was not loud, but it was his. He lifted the card. Basil held the bottom corner so the wind could not audition it for flight. Ivo pressed one pin, then the second, both at the top, both away from their fingers. The card faced the gate. The words waited in the sun, true and readable. Ivo stepped back. His hands tingled. Nothing bad had happened. The brave part was over, and the card was still there, which meant the brave part could rest. Basil pinned the blue swirl beside it with two pins too. The line dipped a little and held.

The neighbor came out with a watering can and stopped. She read without hurrying. Her face did a glad, crinkly thing. She did not make Ivo perform a speech. She lifted a hand and said, "Morning smells better when someone names it. Thank you for the thank-you." Ivo managed a wave that felt bigger than his arm. Basil grinned at the ground and then at his friend. The lemons seemed to smell stronger, as if they had heard their own praise. The cards tapped each other gently, a paper applause.

They left the line up for the afternoon. Once the wind tried again, and the two pins did their calm work. Ivo checked from the window and felt a fresh sprinkle of courage, the kind that grows after the first try. He wrote a second card, not because the first was wrong, but because he had more truth. "The lemon water was sweet. We saved you a cup." Basil helped him hang it at the end, two pins, no fuss. Sharing the line made the courage feel less lonely. The rosemary let go of the earlier flutter and went back to smelling like itself.

When the neighbor left a reply card in their basket, a drawing of a lemon with a smile, Ivo laughed out loud. The sound surprised him and did not hurt. Basil taped nothing. He simply put the reply on the table where supper would see it. The kindness line outside held three cards now, steady as a small chorus. Ivo touched the crayon in his pocket. It had been a tool for bravery, yellow and ordinary and enough.

The lantern by the window glowed softly on the lemon drawing, making the smile look warm. Ivo looked out at the line, where his words still faced the gate, held by two honest pins. Basil poured the saved lemon water into small cups. Courage tasted a little like citrus and a little like relief, and the evening was quiet enough to enjoy both.
