Celine spread a square of soft blue cloth across the table and smoothed it with both palms until the wrinkles gave up. The cloth smelled faintly of clean soap and the cedar basket where it had waited. Dario held a plump handful of white cotton stuffing, light as a cloud and a little tickly on his wrist. "Auntie said her reading chair feels hard in the afternoon," Celine said. "I want the pillow to be a kindness she can lean on." Dario nodded. "Then it should be squishy, but not so squishy that her book slides off." They both smiled at that picture.

Auntie was in the garden, humming, so the corner of the room felt quiet and important. Celine threaded a big blunt needle with yellow thread. The eye of the needle was wide, and still the thread missed twice. Dario held the thread end steady. "There," he said when it finally went through. "My fingers are being a team." They folded the cloth so the bright side faced in, the way Auntie had shown them yesterday, and Celine began a simple running stitch along one edge. The needle made a tiny pop each time it came through. Up, along, down. The sound was as small as rain on a leaf.

Halfway down the side, the stitches grew bigger, then smaller, like footsteps that could not pick a speed. Celine frowned. "These look like they are hopping." She did not tug hard. She rested the needle on a saucer so it would not hide in the cloth. Dario leaned close. "If we pull the thread just a little, they can sit nearer to each other." He pinched the cloth flat. Celine eased the thread until the stitches looked like a tidy path. "Better," she said. "Thank you for holding. My other hand wanted to fly." The yellow thread shone against the blue, a sunny road.

They left one side open, turned the cloth right side out, and poked the corners gently with a finger until they became square and proud. Then came the stuffing. Dario pushed in a puff. It all rushed into one corner and made a lump like a bun. Celine laughed, a kind laugh, not a sharp one. "The pillow is eating sideways." She reached in and loosened the cotton, spreading it with small pinches. More went in, and more, until the pillow felt even when they pressed it. "Squish test," Dario said. They both pressed. The pillow breathed back against their hands, soft and springy. It smelled like cotton and a little like the cedar basket.

The open edge still grinned at them. Celine pinned it shut with two safe clips and began the last line of stitches. This part was slower because the pillow was fat and the cloth wanted to roll. Dario kept the edges meeting neatly. "I can feel the cotton trying to peek," he said. "Tuck it back like a blanket." He tucked. The needle walked to the end. Celine tied a knot the way Auntie had taught, with the thread looped twice, and snipped the tail with scissors only after Auntie, coming in with garden-cool hands, nodded and stood beside her. The snip was quick. The knot stayed.

They brushed loose threads onto a plate and looked at the pillow in the window light. It was not a perfect square. One corner was a bit rounder, and one stitch sat slightly higher than its neighbors, like a bird on a wire. Celine touched that stitch. "I could hide it," she said, then shook her head. "No. It shows where we slowed down and fixed the path. I like knowing that." Dario set the pillow on the reading chair. The chair's wooden arms were smooth and a little cool. The pillow made a soft blue hill in the middle. "It looks ready for a book," he said.

When Auntie came to sit, she did not get a speech. Celine simply said, "We made this so your reading time can feel gentler." Auntie lowered herself into the chair and let out a happy hum that buzzed in her chest. She leaned back. The pillow received her. "Oh," she said, "it holds me like a good pause." She ran a hand over the yellow stitches. "Someone was patient here." Celine felt warm in her face, but she did not say the pillow was the best in the world. She said, "Dario kept the edges together. I kept the needle moving." Dario added, "And you showed us the knot."

They left Auntie with her book and a cup of mint water beading on the side. From the doorway Celine watched the blue pillow stay put, doing its quiet job. Dario whispered, "Kindness can be something you hug without knowing it is a gift." Celine whispered back, "And it can have a funny corner." They washed their hands, the soap slippery and sweet, and put the needle back in its tin. The room smelled of cedar, cotton, and the green air Auntie had carried inside.

The lantern by the window glowed softly beside the reading chair, and the blue pillow caught a little of that gold along its rounder corner. Celine and Dario did not linger to be praised. They listened to a page turn, a soft paper sound, and felt the good tiredness of finished stitches in their fingers. The pillow was done. It was soft. It was a kindness with their hands still hidden in it, and that was enough.
