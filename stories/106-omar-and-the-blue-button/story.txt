The lost-and-found box sat under the sunny school window, its wooden sides worn smooth by many honest hands. Omar lifted the lid, and a smell of wool, eraser dust, and a hint of orange peel rose up. Inside were a striped mitten, a pencil with star prints, and a blue button that shone like a drop of evening sky. The button had four holes and a tiny chip of thread still in one of them. Omar's puppet at home, he thought, then corrected himself, his school puppet on the shelf, had a loose coat. The button looked exactly right. His fingers hovered over it. The want was bright and quick.

Cora knelt beside him with her cardigan folded over her arm. One cuff gaped where a button should have held it. She was not upset in a heavy way. She was searching with calm eyes. "Has anyone seen a blue button?" she asked the room, and then she saw Omar's open hand, because he had already picked the button up without thinking. The button was warm from the wool around it. Omar felt his ears grow warm too. He could have closed his fingers. Instead he opened them all the way, so the blue sat in plain sight on his palm.

"I found it in the box," he said. "For a moment I wanted it for the puppet coat. The coat is loose, and this color is my favorite. But it was waiting here, and waiting means it belongs to someone." His voice stayed steady, even though his heart tapped faster. Honesty felt like standing in a doorway, a little breezy, and then like stepping into a room that fit. Cora's face opened into relief. "That is my button," she said. "The thread matches. I snagged it near the paint cups, and I hoped the box would keep it safe." She did not scold the wanting. She nodded at it. "Wanting can wave hello and still not take."

Omar dropped the button into her cupped hands. It clicked softly against her palm. "I am glad I said the want out loud," he said. "If I had hidden it, the glad feeling would have been smaller." Cora agreed. "And my cuff would still be flapping like a little flag." She tried the button against the threads. It lined up. The sweater wool was fuzzy and smelled like the sunshine it had worn at the fair practice outside. "Could you hold the cuff flat while I twist the thread that is left?" she asked. Omar held the cloth gently. Cora twisted and looped the remaining thread through the holes in a simple cross, the way she had practiced on a scrap. It was not fancy. It held.

"There," she said, flexing her wrist. The cuff stayed closed. "Thank you for checking the box and for telling the truth about the puppet." Omar looked at the puppet shelf across the room. The little coat still gaped. He did not feel a sting, only a plan. "I can make a button from paper and paste until a spare one appears," he said. "A pretend button can be honest about being pretend." Cora smiled. "The art scraps have blue paper." They walked to the scraps together. Scissors whispered. Paste smelled like clean starch. Omar cut a small circle, marked four holes with a pencil dot, and stitched it to the puppet coat with a scrap of yarn, loose enough not to tear the felt.

He held the puppet up to the window. The paper button did not shine like the real one, but it looked cheerful, and it kept the coat together. "This one is mine because I made it," he said. "That one is yours because it came from your cuff." Cora buttoned and unbuttoned once, to hear the soft snap of thread against wool. "The lost-and-found did its job," she said. "And so did you." They wrote a tiny note and taped it inside the lid. "Buttons, mittens, and pencils can rest here until their people come." Omar added, "If you want something you find, say so, and then keep looking for the owner."

They placed the striped mitten on top so it would be seen, and they left the star pencil in a side tray. The box lid closed with a wooden thud that sounded like a promise. Omar's hands smelled faintly of paste. Cora's cuff felt snug at her wrist. Neither of them hurried away. The hallway outside was bright, and the kindness mailboxes down the wall seemed to approve of straight answers.

Omar set the puppet on the shelf where everyone could see the paper button and know it was handmade. He took a breath that filled him all the way to his shoes. Telling the truth had not made him smaller. It had made the room kinder and the coat problem solvable. Cora waved with her mended sleeve. He waved back with the puppet's paw.

The lantern by the window glowed softly on the lost-and-found box, catching a last glint where the blue button used to be and finding nothing missing, only things ready to be claimed. Omar watched the light, then turned toward the art table, already thinking of a second paper button to share if another coat needed one.
