Orhan carried a small notebook with a string tied around it so the wind would have to ask before stealing a page. On the first page he had written four questions in his best letters. Why does the heron stand so still? Why are some clovers purple while the grass is green? Why does Pippin flick one ear? Why do the rabbits pause before they hop? He did not plan to push any answer into a corner. He planned to visit the questions the way you visit neighbors, with time and good manners.

The grown-up came along because pond edges and pony paths were better with company. They stopped first at the viewing rock. The heron was a gray quiet shape in the thin water, one leg tucked, the other holding the morning up. Orhan did not clap. He wrote, He is watching the water light. Then he watched long enough to see the heron change legs with great care. "Stillness lets him notice small things," the grown-up said. "Emeka learned that too." Orhan added, Patience is part of his work. He did not invent a splash. The heron had not offered one.

They took the path that stayed far from the busiest clover. Bees moved on the purple blooms like punctuation marks Orhan could see and not touch. "Maybe purple is easier to find," he said. "Maybe the smell is a map. I do not know yet." He liked borrowing Flora's honest line. He drew a purple dot and a green blade and an arrow labeled bees stay over there. A white clover stood among the purple ones, and he did not pick it. Curiosity that picks everything ends up holding less.

Pippin was in the pasture with the gate open to his shelter, dozing and not dozing. Orhan stood at the rail. One of Pippin's ears flicked toward a distant chicken song, then relaxed. The other ear stayed soft. "The ear is a question he asks the air," Orhan whispered. The grown-up nodded. "He can hear more than we can. A flick can mean I noticed, not I am worried." Orhan wrote that down and underlined noticed. Ioanna's drawing of the oak walk was on the porch at home, and Orhan thought he might ask her later what the ear did when she walked beside the pony. A question could be saved for a person as well as for a page.

The rabbits were at Delphine's shared row, which Orhan knew not to muddle. He sat on the path. A rabbit nibbled, paused, listened, and hopped only a short way to the next clover. The pause was not a fright in this peaceful hedge. It was a check. "They look before they go," Orhan said. "Like Maude taking a breath before she speaks." He smiled at his own comparison. The meadow and the people were teaching with the same gentle pattern.

He did not finish every answer. The notebook's last lines were beautifully unfinished. Why the white flowers waited, why the heron chose that exact reed, why a pony's dream might need one ear awake: I do not know yet. The grown-up said those words were a doorway, not a failure. Orhan closed the book and tied the string.

They passed Button practicing a quiet sit, and Orhan added a fifth question for another day: how does a tail say hello in one wag? He could already guess, and he still wanted to watch.

Orhan read the notebook to Flora on the step, stopping wherever he had written that he did not know yet. Flora did not rush to fill those lines. She only pointed at her pollen drawing and said some answers like to arrive tomorrow. They added a tiny rule at the back of Orhan's book: watch from the path, leave flowers, let the heron be still, let the pony's ear ask the air. The rule was curiosity with good shoes on. It could walk far without stepping on anyone's work.

A rabbit paused at the hedge, checked the evening, and hopped a short hop. Orhan did not follow. He wrote one last line: looking before going is wise for rabbits and for questions. Then he tied the string. The meadow would still be full of wonders at breakfast. He did not need to collect them all before dark. A curious child can go to sleep with a pocket full of maybes and call that a successful day.

The grown-up lit nothing yet and still the room felt ready for thinking. Orhan sharpened his pencil with care and set it beside the string, a tool for tomorrow's first question. He did not open the book again. Curiosity can rest without disappearing. From the window the meadow was one dark green shape full of lives he would greet, not grab, when morning made the colors clear.

The lantern by the window glowed softly beside the notebook on the sill. Orhan read his four questions aloud, some with answers tucked under them, some with space left open like a meadow. Curiosity, he decided, is not a race to the end of the page. It is a careful walk that keeps both the wonder and the neighbor safe.
