The photo album was heavier than Joy expected. She carried it to the kitchen table with both arms and a careful chin. Cousin Omar slid a napkin underneath so the cover would not scratch the wood. Grandpa Ivan opened the first page so slowly that the paper barely whispered. Joy’s knees bounced. She felt curiosity, bright and fizzy, and it wanted every picture at once.

“Who is the baby with the spoon on his nose?” she asked, pointing. Grandpa Ivan laughed. “That is me, practicing breakfast. My mother said the spoon was a drum and my face was the stage.” Omar leaned in. “You had round cheeks,” he said, not unkindly, just amazed. Joy wanted the next page immediately. Her hand hovered. Grandpa Ivan did not scold the hover. He said, “Curiosity is a wonderful guest when it knocks and listens. Ask anything. We will stay with a picture long enough to hear it.”

Joy took a breath and made her hand into a listener instead of a flipper. “Why is the curtain yellow in this one?” she asked. The photo showed a kitchen almost like theirs, with a smaller stove and the same shape of window. Grandpa Ivan touched the corner gently. “Your great-grandma sewed that curtain from a sunny cloth. She said a kitchen should look awake even on gray mornings.” Joy imagined the cloth under fingers. She had not known a curtain could be a family story. The fizzy feeling settled into a warmer glow. She still had questions, and now each one had a chair.

Omar found a picture of children sitting on a floor with pots and wooden spoons. “A band,” he whispered. Grandpa Ivan nodded. “We played a soup song for anyone who would clap. I was shy about the big pot, so I tapped a cup. A small sound still belonged.” Joy looked at Omar. “That is like us, only the socks in the picture are striped.” They giggled. Joy asked what the song’s words were. Grandpa Ivan hummed a line about carrots going swimming in the pot. Joy hummed it back. The album was no longer a stack of paper. It was a door into afternoons.

On the next page a balcony appeared, crowded with pots. Joy squinted. “Is that mint?” Grandpa Ivan said he thought it was, and also a tomato that took all summer. Joy felt a new question rise. “Did you ever feel bored waiting for the tomato?” “Yes,” he said. “Bored and hopeful can share a bench. I watered anyway.” Joy liked that answer so much she repeated it in a whisper so she would remember. Omar asked who held the watering can in the corner of the photo. The person was mostly a sleeve and a smile. “That is my brother,” Grandpa Ivan said. “He visited from another town and learned our balcony by heart.”

Joy noticed something else. In many photos, someone was missing a corner, or a head was cut off, or a blur passed by. She had thought old photos should be perfect. “These are wiggly,” she said. Grandpa Ivan chuckled. “Families move. The wiggle means we were alive and busy. The story is still true.” Joy felt her curiosity grow kinder. She did not need every picture to be smooth. She needed to ask, and to wait for the answer, and to look with soft eyes. She turned the page only after Grandpa Ivan finished a sentence. Omar copied her pace. The three of them made a little team of noticing. Joy asked Omar a question too, so curiosity would not only face backward. “What does your kitchen sound like in the morning?” Omar said it sounded like a kettle and his father’s whistle. Joy nodded as if that sound belonged in the album as well. “I will remember your whistle kitchen,” she said. Omar looked pleased to be part of the asking, not only the listening.

Near the end they found an empty pocket where a photo had not yet been added. Joy’s eyebrows rose. “Is this a mistake?” Grandpa Ivan shook his head. “It is an invitation. Albums grow.” Joy looked at Omar. “We could add today,” she said. They did not hunt for a camera. They drew the moment instead, on a card that fit the pocket. Joy drew the album. Omar drew three cups. Grandpa Ivan drew the yellow-curtain memory as a small square of sun. They slid the card in. It looked proud and new beside the older pages.

Joy closed the album with the same slowness Grandpa Ivan had used at the start. Her fizzy feeling was still there, but it was not pushing. “I have more questions for another day,” she said. “I can keep them in my pocket like that card.” Omar patted the cover. “And I learned your grandpa was a cup drummer.” Grandpa Ivan bowed from his chair. The kitchen felt wider, as if many Sundays were sitting politely around the table, ready to be asked one question at a time.

The lantern by the window glowed softly on the album’s worn corner. Joy rested her hand there and felt the warmth of old paper and new drawings together. Curiosity had not rushed her past the stories. It had given her reasons to stay, to hum, and to leave a pocket of today for someone else to find.
