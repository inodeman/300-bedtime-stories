Anita stood on the grassy hill with the red kite resting like a folded bird in her arms. The afternoon sky was a clear and gentle blue. Luca sat on the path with a wooden spool between his knees. The string was smooth and pale. A few grass stems bent and then stood tall again. Anita tilted her head. She wanted to know how a kite could rise when no hand pushed the cloth from above. She watched the sky, the grass, and the quiet string, and she did not rush to run.

Luca smiled and held the spool steady. "The kite likes a breeze that is polite," he said. "If we tug too hard, the cloth just flaps and falls." Anita liked that idea. A respectful breeze would invite the kite along, not shove it. She set the kite on the grass with its face toward the open sky. Then she crouched and put one palm near the ground. The air felt still, like a room before someone opens a door. She asked Luca how they would know the breeze had arrived. Luca pointed at a low bush. "That bush will tell us," he said. "Leaves are honest."

They waited. A small cloud drifted, no bigger than a sheep, and its shadow slid over their shoes and went on. Anita counted five slow breaths. She noticed a seed puff tremble but not leave its stem. She noticed the string lying in a loose curve, not tight and not tangled. Her curiosity made a little list. Still air. Soft light. A friend close by. A kite ready. Then one leaf on the bush turned its pale side out. Another leaf followed. The grass near Anita's wrist brushed her skin like a whisper. "There," she said softly. "The breeze is introducing itself."

Anita lifted the kite to her chest. Luca let out a little string, just enough for the cloth to feel the air. The first try was wobbly. The kite nodded sideways and kissed the grass. Anita felt a pinch of disappointment, but she did not call the breeze rude. She asked a new question instead. "Did I hold it too flat?" Luca looked closely. "Maybe it needs a small angle, like a hand cupped under a bubble." They tried again. Anita tipped the top of the kite back a little. The breeze slipped under the cloth. The red face rose to her shoulders and hovered, as if it were thinking.

"Look at the tail," Luca said. The ribbon tail lifted in a gentle curve. Anita laughed, but she kept her questions going. She wondered if the air was stronger up high. She let the string slide through her fingers for one arm's length. The kite climbed above the bush. It did not dart. It rose the way a sleepy bird remembers the sky. The string grew faintly tight, a friendly pull, never a yank. Anita asked Luca to trade places so she could feel the spool too. When she held it, the tug was steady, like a small handshake. "The breeze is doing the lifting," she said. "We are only guiding."

They gave the kite more string, then paused to watch. From the hill they could see the harbor water blinking. The kite stood in the blue like a bright red mark. Anita asked what would happen if they ran. Luca shook his head kindly. "On a day like this, running would be louder than the breeze. Let's listen." So they stood still. A warmer puff came, and the kite bowed as if saying thank you, then straightened. Anita noticed that the best moment was not a shout. It was the quiet between the puffs, when the string hummed a tiny note. She put that note on her list of things worth knowing.

When the breeze eased, the kite sank slowly. Anita walked her hands down the string and gathered the cloth before it could tumble. The kite was warm from the sun. She brushed a bit of grass from its cheek. "I thought the sky did the work by magic," she said. "But the grass told us when to begin, and the angle helped, and waiting helped." Luca wound the string in neat loops. "Questions are good kite string," he said. "They keep a wonder from flying away too fast." Anita grinned at that picture. A question could be a string. You held one end, and you let the other end rise.

They sat for a last look at the hill. The bush leaves had settled. The little cloud was gone. Anita told Luca three things she had learned, using her fingers to count. A breeze can be gentle and still be strong enough. Watching small signs saves a kite from a clumsy fall. Sharing the spool makes the flight belong to both friends. Luca added one more idea. The sky looked even kinder when they had paid attention to it. They folded the kite with care, nose to nose, with the tail wrapped like a ribbon on a gift.

The lantern by the window glowed softly while Anita set the folded kite on the chair beside it. The red cloth looked peaceful in the warm light. She touched the string and remembered the tiny hum. Outside, the sky rested in a calm evening color, and the breeze moved the curtain only a little, polite as ever. Anita smiled, already wondering what the leaves would say in the morning, and glad that curiosity had given her such a gentle way to find out.
