The reading rug in the town library was red and a little fuzzy, and it held a square of sun like a blanket folded just right. Jalen had saved a corner with his sweater, a soft heap that meant he was coming right back. In his hands was nothing, because the picture book he wanted, the one with the blue boat and the friendly gull, was already open across Polly's knees. She was tracing the boat's curve with a careful finger. Jalen stopped at the edge of the rug. His saved corner was still there. His plan was not.

He felt a small tug inside, the kind that says, that was my idea first. Then he saw Polly's face, bright and peaceful, and the tug loosened. A book could be a bridge instead of a fence. He stepped onto the rug. It sprang faintly under his socks. "Is the gull still on the second page?" he asked, voice easy. Polly looked up and smiled. "The gull is here, and it has a spot on its beak like a crumb. I was hoping someone would want to see it too." She patted the rug beside her, the same way she always did, making a place. "Your sweater kept a seat warm. Sit, and we can start the page over. I do not mind beginnings."

Jalen sat. Their shoulders did not bump yet. The book smelled like the shelf, dry and calm, and the page was cool when he held one side. Polly held the other. "You can turn when the sentence ends," she said. "I will point at the small things." She pointed to a coil of rope, a yellow cup on the deck, a lantern drawn on the boat that looked a little like the real lantern by the library window. Jalen read the short line under the picture. His voice and her finger moved together. It felt like friendship, not like taking turns in a stiff way, but like passing a cup so both people could drink.

On the next page the boat met a mirror of water. Jalen noticed something Polly had not said yet. "The reflection has one less wave," he said. "See, the drawn water forgot a curl." Polly leaned closer. Her hair smelled faintly of the lemon soap from school. "You are right. It is like a secret the picture told you." She did not sound left out. She sounded glad his eyes were different from hers. "If I had read alone, I might have missed the missing wave." Jalen felt the gladness land in him too. Sharing a page made the page bigger.

They invented a gentle game that was not a race. Each page, one of them would find a sound hiding in the picture, and the other would find a color. Jalen found the gull's cry written as a tiny chee in the corner. Polly found the exact green of the boat's trim and matched it to a leaf outside the window. They whispered the finds. The rug turned into a little island. Other children walked past with soft feet and did not need the island yet. Jalen was glad. Friendship could be two people and a book before it was a crowd.

When they reached the last page, the boat was home at a dock, and the gull sat on a post. Polly closed the book slowly so the air did not puff their bangs. "Thank you for not taking it away," she said. "You could have, because your sweater was here first." Jalen shook his head. "The sweater was saving a spot, not a book. Spots can stretch." He folded the sweater smaller so a third person could fit if someone came. The idea of stretching made him think of the kindness mailboxes, how a note could make room in a day. "Want to write the gull's name in a note for the book pocket?" he asked. Some library books had a little pocket for reader smiles. This one did.

They wrote, "The gull's crumb-beak made us look closer." Jalen's letters were round. Polly added a tiny drawing of the missing wave. They slid the note into the pocket. The paper whispered. Jalen put his sweater on, because the sun square had moved and the rug felt cooler. Polly hugged the book once, then set it on the return cart so another pair of friends could meet the boat. She did not keep it stuck to her lap. That was a kind of friendship too, with the next readers.

They stayed on the rug a minute more, not needing to fill the quiet. Jalen listened to pages turning somewhere in the picture-book bins and to Polly's calm breath. He thought about how the tug inside him had turned into a seat. If he had tugged the book away, he would have had the pictures and lost the pointing finger, the lemon-soap quiet, the missing wave. He liked the trade he had actually made. He had traded alone for together, and together had more color.

The lantern by the window glowed softly across the reading rug, warming the red fuzz where their knees had been. Jalen and Polly stood, shoulders almost touching, and walked to the cart to choose one more book they could hold from both sides.
