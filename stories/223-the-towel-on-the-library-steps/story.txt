Carmen sat on the warm library steps with a dry towel folded on her lap. A short shower had just hurried past, tapping the roof and then moving on. The path steamed gently in the returning sun. Nia stood at the bottom of the steps, holding a picnic basket that had not been opened. Her shoulders were rounded, and her voice was small. "We missed the sunny part," she said. Carmen scooted over and patted the dry stone beside her. "Come sit. The day is not finished. It only changed its coat."

Nia sat, still looking at the basket. Carmen did not tell her to cheer up in a hurry. Kindness, Carmen thought, could be quiet at first. She offered one end of the towel so Nia's hands would have something soft to hold. Together they watched the last drops slip off a leafy branch. Each drop made a tiny bright knock on the step. Carmen pointed, not to correct Nia, but to share. "The shower left gifts," she said. "We can look for them before we decide the picnic is lost."

A color appeared where the sun met the leftover rain. It was faint at first, like a ribbon someone was still painting. Carmen felt her own happy jump, but she let Nia speak first. "Is that a rainbow starting?" Nia whispered. "Yes," Carmen said. "You saw it. You get to name the first color." Nia leaned forward. "Red, at the top, like a strawberry shade." Carmen nodded and kept her own guess tucked away for a moment. Letting a friend go first made the rainbow feel like a gift passed from hand to hand.

They named the bands together, slowly, so nobody skipped a turn. Orange. Yellow. Green. Blue. Carmen was not sure about the last shy stripe, so she asked instead of pretending. "Does that edge look purple to you?" Nia squinted in a cheerful way. "A little violet, like a flower in shade." Carmen smiled. "Then we both found it." Down the path, the wet stones mirrored a piece of the bow. Carmen walked Nia there, matching her pace, because kindness also meant not rushing ahead to the best view and leaving a friend behind.

Near the mirror puddle, Nia sighed, but the sigh was softer. "I wanted sandwiches in the bright sun," she said. Carmen opened the basket only enough to show the cloth inside. "We can still have them here, on the dry step, with the rainbow as our tablecloth in the sky." She gave Nia the first sandwich and the spot with the clearest view. Carmen took the second seat, a little to the side, and she did not mind. The colors were big enough for two. A kind choice, she decided, was a seat that helped someone else see.

While they ate, a breeze turned the leaves and shook down a last silver sprinkle. Nia flinched at the cool drops, then laughed. Carmen held the towel over both of their knees until the sprinkle stopped. "See?" Carmen said. "The shower is really finished. It just wanted to say goodbye." Nia looked up at the rainbow, which had grown brighter. "You stayed with me when I felt disappointed," she said. "That helped the colors arrive." Carmen felt warm in a way that had nothing to do with the sun. Staying close was a simple thing, and it mattered.

They played a gentle game. Nia described the rainbow for a pretend listener who could not see it, and Carmen added only the parts Nia forgot, like the way it seemed to touch the harbor light. Then they switched. Carmen's words were careful, and she left a pause so Nia could add a better one. "It looks like the sky is sharing its crayons," Nia said. Carmen clapped once, softly. "That is the best sentence. We should keep it." She meant it. Kindness could cheer for a friend's idea without grabbing the moment back.

When the rainbow faded, it did not vanish like a broken thing. It thinned into the blue, peaceful and complete. Nia packed the basket while Carmen shook the towel. "Thank you for not leaving me with only a missed picnic," Nia said. Carmen hooked her arm through Nia's in a friendly way. "Thank you for naming the colors. I saw more because you looked with me." They walked toward home under a sky that had done its raining and was now ready to rest. The steps behind them dried in quiet patches.

They lingered on the steps until the path stopped steaming. Carmen pointed out a small arch of color in a puddle, a second rainbow too humble for the sky but perfect for anyone sitting close. Nia gasped and gave Carmen the first naming this time, returning the earlier kindness. "Gold at the near edge," Carmen said, "and a quiet blue by the leaf." Nia nodded as if the words were a present. Sharing the little bow mattered as much as sharing the large one. When they finally stood, both of them felt looked-after, not hurried, and the picnic basket seemed lighter than it had at the start of the shower.

The lantern by the window glowed softly as Carmen and Nia set the picnic cloth beside it to finish drying. The rainbow was gone, but its order lived in their talk, red through violet, shared without a rush. Outside, the evening sky was mild and clear. Carmen watched the lantern light rest on Nia's smile and felt glad that kindness had been as real as any color in the sky.
