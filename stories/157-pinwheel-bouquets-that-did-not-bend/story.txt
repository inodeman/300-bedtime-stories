Tilde stood between two bicycles and a crowd of paper pinwheels that would not sit still. The fair booth on the common needed them before opening, and the common was a short ride from home with Mama. Little brother Otto held one pinwheel and spun it with his chin, which made everyone laugh and made the pile tip. "If they tip, they bend," Tilde said. "Bent pinwheels look tired on a table." Mama nodded. "Then this is a teamwork morning. Three people, two baskets, one plan. Who has the first idea?"

Tilde looked at the pinwheels, red, blue, and cream, each on a slim stick. "If we dump them in, the sticks poke. If I carry them all in my arms, I cannot steer." Otto spun his again. "They want to be flowers in a sleeve." Tilde's eyes widened. "A sleeve. The picnic scarf. We can bundle a few, sticks together, wheels out, like a bouquet that does not sneeze." Mama brought the scarf. "Good idea. Now jobs. Otto, you are the holder of the open scarf. Tilde, you are the placer. I am the tie and the checker. If someone is tired of a job, we trade before we get grumpy."

They tried. The first bouquet was too fat and leaned. Tilde felt the hurry rising. "It is not working." Otto's mouth went small. Mama lifted a hand. "Teamwork includes a second try that is not a blame. What did the bouquet teach?" Tilde touched the fat middle. "Too many. And the wheels need a paper collar so they do not touch and crease." She folded a spare sheet into a loose tube. Otto held the tube as if it were a trumpet. "I can be tube captain," he said. "Yes," Tilde said. "Tube captain is a real job." They slid four pinwheels in, wheels proud at the top, and Mama tied the scarf gently below the paper.

The bouquet stood in the bicycle basket like a small colorful tree. A second bouquet fit in Mama's basket. The rest would ride in a shallow box strapped flat, which Mama had thought of while they tied. "See?" Mama said. "Your plan plus my strap plus Otto's trumpet tube. None of us carried the whole idea alone." Tilde felt the tight place in her chest uncurl. She had wanted to be the captain of everything. Being the placer was plenty, and better, because the pinwheels survived. They walked the bicycles to the lane, helmets on, no racing.

The ride was calm. When a breeze tried to spin the wheels, Otto called, "They are practicing," and Tilde answered, "Practicing is allowed if the sticks stay down." Mama laughed and kept the pace steady. They passed the post box, the hedge, and the entrance to the common. At the booth, Ms. Calder's helper, a quiet man named Mr. Ben, clapped once. "Those arrived in a good mood," he said. "Yesterday a box came looking like a nap." Tilde grinned. "We gave them scarves." They unpacked in the same jobs: Otto held, Tilde placed, Mama checked. The table became a bright field of spin.

Mr. Ben offered them a practice twirl before guests came. Each pinwheel turned and did not scrape its neighbor, because Tilde had spaced them with a hand's width. "The spacing was Mama's extra idea at the end," Tilde told him, because taking all the credit felt like a wobbly basket. Mama added, "The spacing only mattered because the bouquets arrived unbent. That was the children's system." Otto bowed, nearly spinning himself. "Tube captain," he announced. Mr. Ben saluted with a cream pinwheel. Teamwork, Tilde noticed, sounded nice out loud. It was not a secret that one person had been clever.

They did not stay for the whole fair. Home tasks waited, and the ride back was part of the plan. Empty baskets rattled lightly, a happier sound than a rattle full of bent paper. On the lane Tilde said, "I almost said the first try was Otto's fault because he spun one. It was not. The pile was the puzzle." Otto looked pleased to be unblamed. Mama said, "Noticing that is teamwork too. Teams take care of feelings as well as sticks." Tilde stored the sentence beside the scarf knot in her memory.

At home they shook grass seeds off the scarf and folded it. Otto put the paper tubes in the craft box for another day. Tilde drew a quick picture of the job list and stuck it on the fridge with a plain magnet: holder, placer, tie, checker, trade if grumpy. "We could use this when we pack the library bag," she said. Mama agreed. "Any job with many parts likes a team that can swap." The kitchen smelled like soap and a little like the common's dry grass still hiding in their cuffs.

The lantern by the window glowed softly next to one cream pinwheel Mr. Ben had given them as a thank-you, not as a payment. Tilde set it where a breeze from the open latch could turn it slowly. "It still works because we worked," she said. Otto watched the spin with quiet eyes, his captain energy spent in a good way. Mama dimmed nothing. The glow and the turning paper shared the sill, a small fair light that had come home by teamwork and two careful bicycles.
