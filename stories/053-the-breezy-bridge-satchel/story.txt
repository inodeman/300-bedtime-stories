Priya hugged the tall satchel of library leaves against her chest at the near side of the rope footbridge. The leaves were dry, light, and full of drawings that the hollow tree library wanted back before dusk. A warm breeze slid through the oaks and made the satchel's flap tick against its button. Priya's heart ticked a little faster too. She could cross this bridge on ordinary days without a second thought. Today the leaves felt light enough to slip free if she rushed. She planted her boots on the first plank and spoke to herself in a steady voice. "I can do this one step at a time. The leaves are wanted, and so is my calm."

A gentle fox who had helped stack the leaves at the library trotted up and stood at her heel. Priya looked down, grateful. "Will you walk on the side where the breeze pushes?" she asked. "If a corner tries to lift, you can lay a paw near the satchel, not on the drawings." The fox took that side at once. Its shoulder brushed her knee, a solid friendly weight. Priya checked the button, then the soft guide rope someone had added along the rail. It felt fuzzy and sure under her free hand. She nodded. "We are ready. Not fast. Ready."

They started across. The bridge hummed its familiar string-song. Halfway, the breeze strengthened and a single leaf corner peeked from the satchel like a curious ear. Priya stopped, which was a kind of courage too, the courage not to stumble ahead. She tucked the corner in and tightened the flap. "Stay cozy," she told the leaves. Her cheeks were warm, and her fingers tingled, but her feet knew the planks. The fox waited without a fidget. Below them, ferns waved in the same breeze, looking as if they enjoyed the ride. Seeing the ferns play made Priya's mouth remember how to smile.

She began to sing a walking song her cottage kettle seemed to know, a song with no rush in it. "Step, and step, and here we are." The fox's paws found the rhythm. A firefly, startled from a fern by nothing worse than daylight, drifted up and then settled again. Priya watched it land safely and felt her breath copy that soft landing. "We do not have to fight the breeze," she said. "We only have to hold what matters and keep going." She pictured the library table where these leaves belonged, stacked by color, ready for the next reader. The picture pulled her forward more kindly than any worry could push.

Near the far post, the breeze made one last playful tug. The satchel leaned. Priya leaned the other way, knees soft, eyes on her boots. The fox pressed close so the lean had a limit. Nothing tore. Nothing fell. Priya let out a laugh that was mostly relief and a little bit of pride. "We are a good team of two," she said. She stepped onto the moss of the far side and stood still until her heart slowed to a walk. Then she opened the flap just enough to see the drawings safe, bridge and fern and cottage still drawn in neat lines. "All here," she told the fox. "All thanks to stopping when stopping was wise."

The path to the hollow tree was short and golden. Priya did not run, even though running would have felt like showing off. She greeted the young oaks and the acorn caps scattered like buttons. At the library door she knocked with her knuckle, then entered the leafy hush. She set the satchel on the table and unpacked each page, smoothing them flat. The fox who lived among the stacks sniffed the edges and gave a satisfied blink. Priya's shoulders dropped. "The bridge was breezy," she said. "We came anyway." She accepted a drink of cool water from an acorn-cap cup and felt it travel all the way down, a small celebration.

Before leaving, she looked at her paper map and added a tiny note beside the bridge, a word she lettered carefully: steady. It was not a warning. It was a reminder of how she wanted to feel the next time a breeze tested a careful load. The library fox touched its nose to her wrist, a thank-you that needed no speech. Priya scratched the walking fox behind the ear, because that fox had been her side rail. "Come to the cottage step if you like," she said. "There is shade and a spare biscuit." The fox's tail drew a yes in the air.

They took the long way home around the picnic clearing so the breeze could be only a friend again, ruffling fur and hair without a satchel to guard. Priya talked about the drawings she had liked best, especially a page of overlapping ferns that looked like a choir. The fox listened, panting lightly, happy in the warm day. When her mushroom cottage came into view, round and rooted among moss, Priya felt taller than she had at the first plank. Courage had not erased the tick in her chest. It had given the tick a job and a song.

The lantern by the window glowed softly as Priya set her empty satchel on its hook and shook out a last curl of leaf dust. The walking fox curled on the step, biscuit crumbs on its muzzle, the picture of a job well shared. Priya washed her hands and watched the oaks sway without needing to hold anything still. Courage, she thought, was a button checked twice, a song with no rush in it, and a pause in the middle of a bridge until every precious leaf was tucked safely home.
