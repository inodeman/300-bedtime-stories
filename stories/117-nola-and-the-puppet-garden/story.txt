The puppet stage in the town library had a garden to become, and the garden had too many jobs for one pair of hands. A backdrop of leaves wanted holding. A mouse puppet wanted moving. A soft sound of seed pods wanted shaking. Nola stood in the middle with the mouse already on her hand and a paper vine dangling from the other, and the vine kept slipping. Stefan held a jar of real sprouts he had hoped might sit beside the stage like living scenery, but the jar needed a flat place and two steady hands. Abena had the yellow shaker and a list of cues they had written in big letters.

"I keep dropping the vine when the mouse waves," Nola said. She laughed, but the laugh was a little tired. The curtain smelled like cedar and warm cotton. "If I do every job, the mouse will miss its line." Stefan set the jar on a low stool where it could not be kicked, then stepped away from it so he could help. "Jobs can be shared out like paint cups," he said. "One person, one main job, and we can still see each other." Abena tapped the list. "I can be the sound and the cue card. Stefan can be the garden, leaves and vine. Nola can be the mouse's voice and the mouse's wave. The jar can be the jar. It already knows its job, which is growing quietly."

They tried the first scene that way. Nola slipped her free hand out of the vine and let Stefan pinch it to the top of the stage. His fingers were careful with the paper. The vine stayed. Nola gave the mouse a wave that finally looked like a wave and not like a struggle. "The garden is open," she said, clear and glad. Abena shook the yellow shaker in the patient pattern, shake, rest, shake, and the sound sat under the sentence like moss. Stefan rustled a paper leaf with his other hand. The leaf's hush arrived on the rest, not on top of Nola's words. "I can hear everyone," said a friend on the rug. That was the best review a team could hear.

The second scene asked the leaf to travel. Stefan had to walk the leaf from one side of the stage to the other without blocking the mouse. He crouched so his head stayed under the stage line. His knees clicked softly. Nola slowed her line to match his travel. "The leaf is visiting the seed," she said, and she waited until the leaf actually arrived before the mouse nodded. Abena watched their eyes and shook only when Nola blinked the cue they had invented, a double blink that meant now. Teamwork had little signals the way songs had rests. Nobody had to shout a direction in the middle of the story.

A tangle still tried to happen. The vine's end curled down and tickled Nola's wrist, and she giggled, and the giggle was not in the script. Stefan saw the curl and, instead of freezing, tucked the end behind a clip. The clip snapped, a tiny bright sound. Abena turned the snap into part of the garden by giving one extra soft shake, as if a seed had popped. Nola used the giggle. "Even the vine is cheerful," said the mouse. The audience smiled. What could have been a stop became a step, because three people were watching the same story and not only their own piece. Nola felt a warmth in her chest that was better than doing every clever part alone.

They bowed at the end, mouse, leaf, and shaker all dipping together. The bow took practice too. The first bow made the leaf poke the mouse. The second bow had space between them. "Sorry, leaf," Nola said in the mouse voice, and Stefan answered in a leafy whisper, "No harm. I will bow over here." Abena counted the bow out loud in a whisper, "down, up," so they would rise at the same time. The friends on the rug clapped with soft hands. The jar on the stool caught the light and looked like it was clapping with its shine. Nola's arm was tired in a friendly way, and her fingers tingled from good work.

Nola slid the mouse off and wiggled her fingers until they felt like her own again. "My hand only had to be a mouse," she said. "That was plenty." Stefan rolled the paper vine into a loose circle that would not crease the leaves. Abena wiped the shaker and set it in the basket with the cue list clipped to the handle. The list would help the next team remember the blink and the rest. The stage looked calm. The jar went back by the window, where the sprout could lean toward the light it liked.

Nola sat on the rug between her partners. The fuzz warmed her legs. "I thought a good show meant I held every string," she said. "The good show was the one where the strings had people." Stefan nodded. "The leaf was braver when it was my only job." Abena added, "And the sound stayed kind because I was listening, not also waving." They did not rank the jobs. They thanked them. Outside the library window the afternoon stayed bright, and inside, the curtain swayed once and then rested.

The lantern by the window glowed softly beside the sprout jar and the quiet stage. Nola looked at the mouse on its shelf, the vine in its circle, and the shaker in its basket, three jobs sleeping in three right places. She felt the team still sitting with her, even as their hands finally emptied, and that feeling was the part she wanted to keep.
