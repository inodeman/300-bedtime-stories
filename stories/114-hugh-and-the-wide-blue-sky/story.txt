Three cups of blue sat on the art table, each one a different song of the same color. One was deep as an evening puddle, one was bright as a bird's egg, and one was so pale it looked like sky mixed with milk. Hugh had the wide brush and a long sheet of paper taped to the wall at kid height. He wanted to paint the whole sky himself, edge to edge, because the school fair needed a backdrop for the puppet stage. Victor and Mei stood nearby with smaller brushes and willing hands. The room smelled like wet paper and lemon soap. Sun from the window laid a real sky over Hugh's paper sky, as if the day were offering a model.

"I can do the big parts," Hugh said, and he meant it kindly, but his arms already imagined all the fun. He dipped the wide brush. The deep blue loaded with a satisfying weight. He swept a band across the top. It looked grand. Then he saw Victor's fingers curl and uncurl, and Mei's eyes follow the brush the way a kitten follows a string, full of hope. A backdrop that was only one person's joy might look lonely, even if the color was pretty. Hugh paused with the brush dripping a polite drop back into the cup. "This sky is wider than my arms," he said. "If I share the cups, it can have weather and birds and a moon hiding in the day. Will you take a band each?"

Victor's face opened. "I can paint the middle with the egg-blue, where clouds live." Mei nodded. "I can do the low sky, the pale one that sits on the hills." Hugh felt the wanting loosen into a better shape. Sharing did not erase his wide brush. It gave the wide brush neighbors. "I will keep the top, and I will pass the cups so nobody has to reach across wet paint," he said. He set the deep cup by his feet on a tray, the middle cup by Victor, the pale cup by Mei. Their brushes made three soft sounds, whisk, whisk, whisk. The paper darkened and lightened in stripes that could have looked like a fence, so Hugh showed them how to feather the edges, dragging a clean damp brush along the borders until the bands held hands.

"The feathering is the sharing part you can see," Mei said. She liked when an idea became visible. Victor added a cloud with a drier brush, the white of the paper left bare in a puff. "Is it all right that my cloud covers a little of your top?" he asked. Hugh looked. The cloud made the deep blue look deeper. "Yes. Your cloud is a guest I wanted." He added a tiny V of birds with the tip of a small brush he had thought he would not need. The birds flew across all three bands, which meant the sky was one place, not three kingdoms. Mei painted a low hill using green from another cup, just a hum at the bottom, and asked before the green touched the blue. "Please," Hugh said. "Hills give the sky a lap to sit in."

They worked until the backdrop shone wet and complete. Hugh's shoulder felt used in a good way, not overworked, because he had not done every stroke. He rinsed the wide brush and watched the water turn into a river of evening, then egg, then milk, then clear. The sink sang. Victor wiped a blue thumb on his smock, a badge he seemed proud of. Mei stepped back so far she bumped the reading rug and laughed. "From here it is one sky," she said. "You cannot see the arguments that never happened." Hugh laughed too. "That is my favorite kind of picture."

They guided the backdrop's lower edge while it stayed taped, fanning it gently so the shine would dry even. The moving air cooled Hugh's face. He thought about the fair and the puppets that would stand in front of this blue. The puppets would look like they had room to breathe. If he had kept the brushes, the sky might have been bold and also a little tight, the way a hug is tight when only one person remembers to hug. This sky was a loose, friendly hug. He said a simpler version out loud. "It feels big enough for the whole show." Victor and Mei hummed yes.

Hugh wrote a small card to tape beside the backdrop once it dried. "Sky painted by Hugh, Victor, and Mei. Cups shared on purpose." He read it to them to make sure the names felt right. They each nodded at their own name and at the others. The card stock was creamy and smelled like the kindness notes. Hugh tucked it under a clean cup so it would not blow away. On the tray the three blues sat lower than before, still enough for touch-ups, a little left for the next friend. Leaving some was part of sharing too.

He washed his hands until the creases of his knuckles gave up their color. The towel was warm from the sun on the hook. Through the window he could see the real sky doing the same work, deep up high, paler near the roofs, with one cloud visiting. He pointed, and the three of them looked, quiet and pleased, as if they had copied the day and the day approved.

The lantern by the window glowed softly under the painted sky, a small gold moon they had not planned and did not need to add, because the real light was already sharing itself. Hugh, Victor, and Mei stood in that glow, brushes clean, watching blue turn from shiny to soft.
