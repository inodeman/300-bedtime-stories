The neighborhood bakery had two soup pots singing on the stove and one bread oven ready to chime. Emile stirred the pot on the left, where orange peels floated like little boats. Nkechi watched the pot on the right, full of a cinnamon-sweet vegetable soup that smelled like Saturday. Tomas stood by the oven with a thick cloth in his hands, waiting for the loaves. The shared table was already set with bowls. Everything was ready, and everything wanted a turn at the same minute.

The oven bell gave a bright ting. At the same moment both soups needed a stir so the bottoms would stay silky. Emile turned toward the oven. Nkechi turned toward Emile. Tomas reached for a spoon and a cloth at once. Their elbows met in a soft bump. A spoon clinked. Nobody was hurt. Everybody laughed, and then everybody said, "I will do it," at the same time.

"If we all do everything," Nkechi said, "we will make a tangle."

Emile looked at the pots. The orange boats were drifting to one side. "And if we freeze, the soup will sit too still."

Tomas peered through the oven window. The crusts were the right gold, not a moment past. "We need a pattern," he said. "Like a song with parts."

They made one. It was simple enough to remember with their feet. Nkechi sang, low and steady, "Stir, stir, pass the spoon." Emile answered, "Peel boats, turn them round." Tomas added, "Oven cloth, loaves come down." They tried the song slowly, the way a new recipe likes to be read the first time. Patience kept the notes from piling up.

Nkechi stirred the cinnamon pot three calm circles and passed the long spoon. Emile caught it, turned the orange boats, and nodded to Tomas. Tomas lifted the first loaf onto the rack. The crust crackled in a friendly way. Steam rose. He did not grab the second loaf until the song came back to his line. The second loaf landed safe and square.

"Again," Emile said, smiling. "The pots like the chorus."

They went around twice more. Stir, turn, lift. The kitchen stopped feeling like a rush and started feeling like a dance with flour on its shoes. A picnic basket waited by the door for market loaves, but they did not stuff it yet. Teamwork included finishing the song before the next errand.

When the pots were smooth and the loaves were resting, they carried everything to the shared table. Bowls caught the light. Honey jars stood in a row in case someone wanted a sweet side with the bread. Orange peels from the soup were set on a plate, glossy and bright, ready to be nibbled or saved for glaze. There was more than enough. The extra loaf stayed whole so the table would look generous.

"Your part made my part possible," Nkechi told Tomas.

"Your stir kept my loaf from being the only thing I could think about," Tomas said.

Emile ladled soup and listened to the ladles click in rhythm. "We could have bumped all morning," he said. "The song gave our hands their own roads."

They ate together. The cinnamon soup was warm and round. The orange soup was sunny at the edges. Bread pulled apart in soft clouds. They did not trade jobs in the middle of a bite. They had already done the trading. Now they could taste what the pattern had protected.

Afterward they wrote the turn song on a recipe card and propped it between the pots. The words were short. The steps were clear. Next Saturday, if the bell and the bubbles arrived together, the card would remind them how to share the minute.

A market voice drifted through the open window, calling that the stalls were bright and the morning was wide. Emile waved, but he did not run. The basket got its loaves when the loaves were ready. Nkechi wiped the spoons. Tomas folded the oven cloth. The kitchen exhaled.

After the spoons were wiped, they walked the kitchen once without carrying anything, just to feel the clear paths. The spoon hung on its hook. The oven cloth rested on its peg. The bowls made a quiet stack. Emile tapped the left pot and heard a gentle blub. Nkechi tapped the right pot and heard a softer blub, like a drum under a blanket. Tomas looked through the oven window at the warm, dark racks and smelled the last of the crust and cinnamon. They were not fixing a new problem. They were admiring the pattern that had kept the morning gentle. The picnic basket by the door already held its loaves and a wrapped bowl of the orange soup. Nkechi touched the copy of the turn song tied at the handle and smiled. If the stall got busy, the card would remind them that hurry could wait its turn.

The lantern by the window glowed softly above the card with the turn song. Emile, Nkechi, and Tomas sat with soup steam curling past their cheeks. The pots rested. The oven ticked as it cooled. Their elbows had learned the space between them, and the shared table held a meal that teamwork had kept gentle, hot, and plenty.
