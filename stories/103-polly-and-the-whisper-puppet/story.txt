Behind the small puppet stage in the town library, Polly held a gray mouse puppet and listened to her own breath. The curtain was soft cotton the color of oatmeal, and it smelled faintly of the cedar shelf where the puppets lived. Out on the reading rug, picture books lay in a fan. Jalen was setting paper leaves along the front of the stage for a tiny garden. Polly wanted to be the mouse who invited everyone to look, but when she tried her line, it came out as a thread of sound. "Welcome," she whispered, and even the whisper seemed to hide in the curtain folds.

Jalen peeked around the side, his owl puppet tucked under his arm. The owl had button eyes that caught the window light. "I heard a hello," he said. "It was small, but it was a hello." Polly's cheeks felt warm from trying. "My voice is hiding," she said. "If I speak a bit bigger, it might wobble." She stroked the mouse's felt ear. It was fuzzy and a little worn from many kind hands. "The mouse is brave. I am still borrowing brave." Jalen nodded toward the rug. "We can practice where the only audience is the rug and the dust in the sun. Dust does not mind a wobble."

They sat cross-legged on the red rug. It felt softly scratchy against Polly's ankles in a friendly way. She held the mouse up. "The garden is open," she said, a bit louder than a secret and quieter than a call. The words shook once and then settled. Jalen lifted the owl. "Who is planting the quiet flowers?" he asked, and his owl voice was gentle and round and slow. Polly laughed, and the laugh made the next line easier. "I am," said the mouse. "I brought seeds the size of crumbs." They did the two lines again. The second time, Polly's voice stayed for the whole sentence.

"What if the show is a duet?" Jalen asked. "The mouse never has to fill the room alone. The owl can answer, and the answers make a path." Polly liked that. Courage, she thought, might be a path and not a jump. They planned three short scenes. In the first, the mouse peeked out and said one clear sentence. In the second, the owl asked a kind question. In the third, they both shook paper leaves so the garden sounded like a breeze. Polly practiced the peek until her arm felt steady. The curtain brushed her wrist like a calm reminder.

When two friends wandered over from the picture books, Polly felt her stomach do a small hop. She looked at Jalen. He lifted the owl just enough to be seen. Polly sent the mouse through the curtain opening. Sun from the high window made the felt glow. "The garden is open," she said. Her voice was not huge. It was clear, and clear was what the sentence needed. The friends leaned in, smiling, the way people lean toward a cup of warm cocoa. Jalen's owl asked, "May we see the quiet flowers?" Polly answered, "Yes. Come close. They like gentle eyes, and so do I."

They shook the paper leaves. The sound was a hush-hush, like turning pages. Nobody asked Polly to be louder. The friends clapped with soft hands so the puppets would not tumble. Polly's heart slowed into a steady beat she could feel in her fingertips inside the mouse. She added a new line she had not practiced. "Thank you for listening with near ears." It fit. Jalen gave a tiny happy hoot, still gentle. The show ended with both puppets bowing, their heads tapping once, a soft thump of felt on felt.

Afterward Polly sat on the rug and drank water that tasted cool and clean. Her voice felt a little used, like a pencil after a drawing, and that was a good feeling. "I thought brave meant loud," she said. Jalen shook his head. "Brave can be a true small voice that stays till the end of the line." Polly slipped the mouse back onto the cedar shelf, nose out, so the next friend would see a puppet who had already been courageous today. She patted the shelf. It smelled like pencils and quiet wood.

She helped Jalen gather paper leaves that had drifted onto the rug. One leaf had a crease, and she smoothed it with her palm. They tucked the leaves into a basket for the next garden. Polly tried her line one more time, just for the basket. "The garden is open." The words came out steady. She smiled at the sound of herself. The basket rustled back, as if it agreed.

On the way to the picture-book corner she kept one hand near her throat, not to hold the voice in, but to remember it was there. Jalen walked beside her with the owl until the owl needed its hook. They did not rush. The library floor was cool through their socks, and the sunny squares of window light felt like stepping stones. Polly chose a book with a garden on the cover and held it the way she had held the mouse, carefully and with both hands.

The lantern by the window glowed softly behind the puppet stage, turning the oatmeal curtain a warmer gold. Polly looked back once and saw the mouse's ear in the light. She touched her own smile, surprised and glad, and carried her garden book to the rug, ready to read it in the same clear, unrushed voice.
