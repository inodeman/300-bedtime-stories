Hawa liked making things that did not need a store. She could make a paper fan, a pebble picture, or a song that lasted only as long as the chore it helped. On this morning the chore was oats for the speckled chickens. The oats were the right food, measured in a cup, never dumped in a heap that would spoil. Hawa looked at the plain bowls and thought, plain can be the start of an idea.

"May I decorate the outsides?" she asked. The grown-up said yes, if the paint was the washable kind kept for outdoor pots, if it dried completely, and if no paint went near the rim where beaks might touch. Hawa agreed to every rule before she opened the little tin. Rules did not shrink her idea. They gave it a frame, like a border drawn around a picture and not a wall.

She painted dots the color of marigolds and leaves the color of the meadow after rain. She did not paint eyes or words that might confuse anyone. The bowls looked like small gardens turned inside out. While they dried in the sun, upside down on a clean cloth, Hawa worked on the second half of her plan. Breakfast needed a song that meant you may come if you like, not a song that meant hurry.

She tried a tune under her breath. "Oats in the bowl, oats in the bowl, come if you wish, the morning is whole." The notes were simple. Gideon, who was brushing the greeting stone with a whisk, listened. "It sounds like a gate opening softly," he said. Hawa took that as a good review. She sang it again, slower, so it would not startle even a feather.

When the bowls were dry, Hawa checked the rims with a clean thumb. Smooth. No paint. She set them in the usual breakfast place, spaced so each chicken could have room. She did not hide behind a corner for a surprise. Surprises were not her gift today. She stood where the chickens could see her, and she sang her song once, hands still.

The speckled hens came around the rosemary in their own line. They knew oats. They knew Hawa's calm feet. They did not know the dots yet. The first hen paused, tilted her head at a marigold spot, and decided the bowl was still a bowl. She ate neatly. The others joined, murmuring. Hawa felt the special feeling of a made thing being used. Creativity, she thought, is not only for walls and paper. It can sit on the ground and hold breakfast.

She sang the song once more at the end, as a thank-you, then stopped so the yard could have its regular sounds. A chicken scratched once, found the dust patch, and began her bath. The bowls were not hats and not hurdles. They were just brighter. Hawa collected them when they were empty, washed them, and set them to dry again. Making something included taking care of it afterward.

In the afternoon she taught the song to Corin, who wanted a tune for picnic walking. They changed one line together. "Steps in the lane, steps in the lane, light in the basket, Moss is not strained." Hawa liked that a song could travel and still be hers at the start. She wrote both versions on a card.

Hawa carried the dry bowls to the shelf and made a second tiny creation that the chickens would never have to eat or wear. She folded a paper chick from a scrap, gave it marigold dots to match the bowls, and stood it beside the song card. It was a joke for people, not a muddle for hens. Gideon looked in and said the paper chick had excellent quiet-hello posture. Hawa laughed and sang one last soft line, then stopped, because even a good song should know when the yard has heard enough. Creativity, she decided, includes knowing when to put the brush down and let the ordinary morning be the star.

She checked the rims one last time in the fading light, a maker's careful habit, and found them as clean as when she had begun. The marigold dots had dried even and bright. Hawa set a reminder pebble on the song card so she would not forget to sing softly rather than loudly in the morning. A new idea did not have to surprise the chickens to be real. It only had to welcome them. She hummed the welcome under her breath all the way to the door, then let the last note fade before she stepped inside. Inside, she thought of the clean oats cup waiting where Jasper could find it, a quiet thank-you for the true spill story, and of the little space left on the shelf so the paper chick would not be crowded by tomorrow's bowls. Making room, she thought, is the last gentle touch a new idea needs.

The lantern by the window glowed softly above the card with its two little songs. Hawa hummed the chicken one while the yard settled. Tomorrow she might add a single new dot, or she might leave the bowls exactly as they were. A creative idea did not have to grow bigger every day. Sometimes it only had to be kind, finished, and ready for morning.
