Benji stood over the smaller soup pot and sniffed so hard his scarf slipped. The neighborhood bakery smelled like cinnamon bread, and under that smell lived the soup, full of vegetables and a friendly herb scent. Ayo held a strip of orange peel. Bea held the recipe card, which said the peel could go in at the beginning. A tiny pencil note in the margin said, or maybe later. The word maybe made Benji's curiosity sit up.

"Why does later matter?" he asked. "Peel is peel."

Ayo turned the strip. The bright side flashed. "Maybe heat changes it. Maybe time changes it. We could find out without using up the pot."

The big pot was already full for the shared table, abundant and calm. They ladled two small cups from it, little samples, and left the rest steaming under the lid. Nothing about the test made the meal smaller. Bea set the cups on the counter between the bread ovens and a row of honey jars.

"Same soup," she said. "Two questions."

In the first cup, Ayo slipped the peel in while the soup was still going to simmer a long while on a quiet burner. They waited. Waiting was part of the answer. Benji wanted to sniff every few breaths. Bea reminded him that a question needs a fair chance. They counted oven ticks. They watched a cinnamon stick, set aside for the buns, and did not stir it into the test by accident.

In the second cup, they kept the peel out until the soup was hot and ready. Then Bea twisted the peel so a mist of oil shone on the surface and dropped the curl on top. The smell leaped up at once, sunny and quick.

They carried both cups to the shared table, where picnic baskets were parked underneath and the Saturday light fell in squares. They sniffed the long-simmered cup first. The orange had become soft, round, and mixed in, like a color blended on a wet page. They sniffed the fresh-peel cup. The orange was bright, a trumpet next to the soup's drum.

"They are relatives," Benji said. "Not twins."

Ayo tasted the soft one with a small spoon. "This tastes like the peel went on a long walk and came home cozy."

Bea tasted the bright one. "This tastes like the peel just knocked on the door." She laughed. "I like both knocks."

They traded spoons and tried again, slowly, so their noses could reset. Curiosity did not mean gulping. It meant noticing. Benji closed his eyes. In the first cup he found a warm hush. In the second he found a sparkle. Cinnamon from the bakery air sat around both, a sweet frame.

"The card can tell both truths," Ayo said. "Early peel for a cozy pot. Late peel for a bright cup. If we hide one, the next cook will think there is only one door."

Bea wrote it clearly. She added, There is plenty of soup either way. Taste a small cup before you choose, and share the choice. Benji drew two tiny peels, one pale and one vivid. They propped the card against the honey jar so the light could bless the words.

Then they poured their sample cups back into bowls, not to waste a drop, and ladled more from the big pot for anyone who sat down. The table could offer both styles because the pot was generous. A late peel went onto one serving bowl like a bright hat. An early-simmered ladle filled the other bowl with the cozy version. Bread from the oven, still crackling, sat between them. Honey waited for the bread, not for the soup, and that was a cheerful boundary.

Neighbors from the market did not need names to enjoy the smell. They followed it in, and Benji explained in a short, excited way. Early is a walk. Late is a knock. Which do you like? People chose. Some chose both. The pot did not mind.

Ayo rinsed the sample cups. Bea capped her pencil. Benji leaned over the card one more time to be sure the maybe had become a clear pair of steps. The ovens ticked. The market chimed. Orange oil still perfumed his fingers.

"I thought a recipe was one path," he said.

"It can be two paths and one pot," Bea said.

Ayo nodded. "And a question is a good spoon."

Benji sniffed his sleeve and laughed because the bright peel had followed him. Ayo brushed a crumb from the card so the two paths stayed readable. Bea ladled a cozy bowl and a bright bowl for the center of the table, then set bread between them like a bridge. They invited their own spoons to take turns, one sip cozy, one sip bright, with a pause so the nose could rest. The pause made the difference even clearer. Early peel was a blanket. Late peel was a window opened. Both belonged in a Saturday kitchen where the pot was deep and the ovens were kind. Curiosity, they decided, was not a rush to be first. It was a second cup, a second smell, and a pencil willing to write both answers down.

The lantern by the window glowed softly over the two bowls and the rewritten card. Benji, Ayo, and Bea sat with orange on their fingers and cinnamon in the air. Curiosity had not spoiled the soup. It had opened another door, and the shared table had room for both answers.
