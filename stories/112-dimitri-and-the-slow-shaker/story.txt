Shakers waited in a basket in the sunny music corner, each one a small cup with a lid and a secret of beans or buttons inside. Dimitri picked up the red shaker and gave it a fast, happy burst of sound. Chickachickachicka filled the room and bounced off the paint cups. It was exciting for two breaths, and then it covered the song Hugh was trying to hum. Hugh did not frown. He held his own blue shaker still against his chest so it would not join the storm by accident. The air smelled like wood and the orange oil someone used on the shelves.

"I want to match the picture-book rain," Dimitri said, a little out of breath. "The page says soft rain on leaves, and my hands did a gallop." He shook again, slower for a moment, then the gallop came back, because his elbows liked speed. Hugh nodded. "Speed is a color we can use later. This song asks for patience. Listen to the space between sounds." He shook once, then rested his hand open, then shook once more. The blue shaker said chick, quiet, chick. It sounded like drops deciding where to land. Dimitri's shoulders listened before his ears finished. "There is a rest I keep skipping," he said. "The rest feels like nothing, so I rush to fill it."

"The rest is a something," Hugh said. "It is the part that lets the first chick sit down." He tapped Dimitri's wrist lightly, a metronome made of kindness. Together they tried four beats. Shake, rest, shake, rest. Dimitri's second try still crowded the rest. He laughed at himself without being unkind to himself. "My beans are in a hurry to see the world." Hugh grinned. "We can give them a job they like. On the rest, we touch the shaker to our knee so our hands remember to pause." The knee touch was cool plastic against cloth. It worked. Dimitri's next pattern held. The room stopped feeling crowded. The hum Hugh had saved could finally sit on top of the chicks like a bird on a fence.

They opened the picture book to the rain page. The illustration showed leaves with single drops, not a blur. Dimitri matched one shake to one drawn drop. When the page had six drops, he played six, with rests fat enough to taste. Patience turned out to have a flavor, like waiting for a sip of water until you were truly thirsty, then enjoying it more. He said so, and Hugh agreed. "If we shake the whole time, no one hears the song. If we wait, the song has a shape." They played the page twice. The second time felt like walking down a known path, pleasant and not boring, because the sounds were round and clear.

A third shaker, yellow, still sat in the basket. Dimitri wanted to grab it and play both. His hands twitched toward it. Then he put the want on the table in words. "I want the yellow one too, and I think two will make me fast again." Hugh thought. "We can add it after the pattern is strong. One hand keeps the rest on your knee. The other hand joins only on the shake." They tried. The yellow shaker had buttons, a brighter tick. At first Dimitri added an extra tick. He stopped himself, breathed, and felt his feet on the floor. The floor was steady. He tried again. Knee, tick-tick, knee, tick-tick. The buttons and the beans agreed with each other. Hugh's hum found a harmony underneath, low and easy.

They carried the pattern to the puppet stage, not for a show, just to hear it against the curtain. The oatmeal cloth softened the sound into something even kinder. Dimitri smiled so wide his cheeks felt round. "The rain is visiting, not stomping," he said. Hugh set a paper leaf on the stage and let it tremble only a little when the shakes were close. "See, the leaf can stay. That means our rain is the friendly kind." They played while looking at the leaf, which gave their eyes a calm job so their hands would not invent extra beats. It was a trick patience could use, a place to look.

When they returned the shakers to the basket, Dimitri lined them up red, blue, yellow, lids tight so the secrets would not spill. He patted the red one. "You are fun when you gallop, and you are beautiful when you walk," he told it. Hugh wrote a small card for the basket. "Rain pattern, shake rest shake rest. Knee touch helps." Other friends could borrow the patience without borrowing anyone's instrument forever. Dimitri's arms felt pleasantly tired, the way arms feel after careful work, not after a scramble. He hummed the shape with no shaker at all and heard the rests in his head, which meant they had moved in and lived there.

He sat on the rug for a moment and let the quiet be the last part of the song. The quiet was not empty. It was earned. Hugh sat too, blue shaker in his lap like a sleeping pet. Neither of them needed to fill the air. The paint cups gleamed. Somewhere a seed jar ticked as it cooled in the window shade. Dimitri noticed he was not itchy to grab a sound. That noticing was the real music lesson.

The lantern by the window glowed softly in the music corner, keeping time with nobody rushing it. Dimitri watched the glow and tapped one finger on his knee, then let the finger rest, proud of the space he could finally leave open.
