Yellow paint shone in the last full cup on the art table, thick and sunny as a slice of mango. Victor held his brush above a fair poster that still needed a sun. The bristles smelled a little like the lemon soap from the sink, because he had rinsed them well. Around him the sunny school hummed with soft work. Paper whispered. A chair leg gave a small squeak. Salma stood at the next seat with a poster of the library steps, and her sky was finished except for the round sun in the corner. She looked at the cup, then at Victor, and her brush hovered too.

"Is that the last yellow?" Salma asked. Her voice was hopeful and careful at the same time. Victor nodded. The paint surface showed a tiny swirl where he had stirred it. "I need a whole sun," he said. "Big rays. The fair banner wants to look warm." Salma touched the edge of her paper. "I only need a coin of yellow. Without it my steps look like they lead into a gray day." She did not reach for the cup. She waited, and the waiting was polite as a hand raised quietly.

Victor felt the wanting in his fingers. A big sun would use a lot. A coin of sun would use a little. He thought of the kindness mailboxes and how a small note still filled a box. Sharing might be like that, a piece that did not empty the goodness. He slid the cup to the middle of the table, where the wood was dotted with old happy specks of color. "We can both dip," he said. "You take the coin first, so the little sun is sure. I will use what is left for rays." Salma's shoulders eased. "Thank you," she said. "I will not swirl deep. Just the top, where the paint is brightest."

She dipped the tip of her brush. The yellow kissed the bristles and gleamed. On her poster she made a neat circle above the steps, then short rays like welcoming fingers. The paint smell rose, sweet and clean. Victor watched the cup. There was still a good pool. He loaded his brush and painted a wider sun on his poster, leaving a few rays unpainted so the yellow would stretch. "Your sun looks like it knows people's names," he said. Salma laughed softly. "Your sun looks like it has room for more friends."

The pool lowered. Victor's last ray came out pale, more like lemonade than mango. He stopped before the brush scratched the cup bottom. "I think the cup is tired," he said. Salma studied both posters. "What if we mix a new gold for the last bits? There is a drop of red in the tiny cup, and white in the cloud cup." Victor liked experiments that were gentle. They asked the colors, in a way, by testing a dot on scrap paper. A drop of red, a drop of white, a spoon of the remaining yellow. They stirred with a craft stick. The new color came up like toast with honey.

"Gold for the edges," Salma said. They each traced the rims of their suns. The gold made the yellow look even brighter, the way a lantern edge makes light look round. Victor added gold freckles on the library steps of Salma's poster, tiny dots like warm stones. Salma added one gold ray to Victor's sun that he had skipped. "Now it is ours, but also still yours and mine," she said. Victor nodded. The posters did not match, and that was good. They looked like neighbors.

They carried the posters to the drying shelf near the window, walking slowly so the wet suns would not run. Air from the window touched Victor's face, cool on his cheeks and warm on his arms where the sun itself reached. The shelf paper crackled under the posters. While they waited, they rinsed brushes until the water went from sunrise-colored to clear. The sink gurgled. Victor set the empty-enough yellow cup upside down to dry, and it made a soft ceramic tick.

"I thought sharing meant my sun would be smaller in a sad way," Victor said, wiping his hands. The towel was rough and clean. "It is smaller than my first plan, and it is better. It has your ray in it." Salma tapped her poster frame, away from the wet paint. "And my day is not gray. The steps have a sun that was almost only yours. I like that we can see the gift." They decided the fair table would show the posters side by side, with a little card that said, "Paint shared on purpose."

Victor peeked once more at the gold edges. They had begun to dull from wet shine to a soft glow as they dried, still pretty, just quieter. He felt proud without needing to be first or only. Salma hummed a small tune she had learned with shakers, slow and even. The art room answered with paper sounds and the far clink of jars on the science table.

The lantern by the window glowed softly above the drying shelf, and the two suns seemed to answer it. Victor and Salma stood with clean hands and paint-freckled smocks, watching the gold settle. There was no last cup left to argue over. There was a shared light, still wet at the center, already warm at the edges.
