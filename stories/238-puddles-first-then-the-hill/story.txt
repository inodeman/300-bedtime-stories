Rina and Carmen stood where the lane met the meadow after a short shower. The sun was back, and a rainbow rested over the far trees, bright and gentle. Wet stones shone. The air smelled like leaves that had just had a bath. Rina pointed along the lane. "Let's follow the puddles. They hold pieces of the bow." Carmen pointed toward the hill. "Let's go up. From there we can see the whole rainbow at once." They both wanted a real walk with a real friend. For a moment the path seemed to split their wishes.

Rina felt her feet aim one way and her heart tug the other, because she did not want to leave Carmen behind. Carmen felt the same tug. Friendship, they had learned on the library steps, meant staying with the person, not only with the best view. Rina spoke first. "What if the rainbow is big enough for two walks, if we take them together?" Carmen's face opened. "Your way first, then mine. We keep our hands free and our plans linked." They hooked pinkies for a second, a small promise, and turned down the lane.

The puddles were excellent mirrors. One held a red curve. Another held green. Rina knelt and showed Carmen how a ripple could erase the color and how waiting brought it back. Carmen did not rush her up the hill. She waited through the ripple. "Your walk is showing me the rainbow's footprints," she said. Rina beamed. Being believed in your idea was a warm feeling. They stepped around the deepest water so flowers near the pots would stay peaceful. The sky's bow remained, patient as a friend who does not mind a slow pace.

At the end of the lane, Rina said, "Your turn." She meant it. She did not add a long extra stop. Carmen led them up the grassy path, matching steps so Rina could walk beside her and not behind. From the hill, the rainbow was one complete arch, a quiet bridge of color. Carmen whispered, "There. This is the picture I wanted to share with you, not keep." Rina looked until she felt full. "It is even better because I saw the pieces first," she said. "The pieces and the whole belong together. Like us on this walk."

They sat on the dry part of the hill and told what each path had given. Rina's path gave close color, surprise depth, and the lesson of a ripple. Carmen's path gave the full shape and a feeling of calm bigness. Neither path had been a waste. Friendship had room for both, the way the sky had room for every band. A respectful breeze lifted their hair and did not chill them. The shower was truly over. The town below looked washed and bright. They passed a water bottle back and forth, sharing sips and the view in the same easy rhythm.

On the way down they invented a rule for next time. If they wanted different good things, they would do both, in an order they chose together, and they would not call either wish silly. Carmen suggested they say the order out loud. Rina did. "Puddles, then hill." Carmen repeated it. "Puddles, then hill." The repetition made the plan feel sturdy. They laughed about how a rainbow had refereed their choices without saying a word. It had simply stayed long enough for two kinds of looking. That seemed like excellent manners in a sky.

Near the library steps, the bow began to fade. Rina checked Carmen's face to see if sadness would land. Carmen looked peaceful. "It stayed for both of our walks," she said. "That is a good visit." Rina agreed. They did not blame the sky for leaving. A visit could end and still be complete. They wiped a wet leaf off the step and sat for a minute where their afternoon had a memory of towels and shared colors. The friendship felt as arched and as solid as the bow had been, bending across two ideas without breaking.

They walked the rest of the way home telling the story again, Rina taking the puddle chapter and Carmen taking the hill chapter. When one forgot a detail, the other added it gently. The lane dried in patches. The meadow shone. Neither child walked ahead to win. Side by side was the whole point, and the rainbow had been their bright excuse to practice it.

They stopped at a bench halfway between lane and hill and drew the rainbow in the condensation on their water bottle, a tiny bow they could both see at once. Rina traced the puddle path as a dotted line. Carmen traced the hill path as a smooth curve. The two lines met at a small star, meaning we stayed together. They liked the map because it proved friendship did not have to pick a single favorite. When the real rainbow faded, the bottle map still shone. They would rinse it later. For now it was a souvenir of choosing both wishes with the same friend.

The lantern by the window glowed softly as Rina and Carmen paused beneath it to finish the retelling. The glass held a little of the evening sky, clear and calm, with no need for more rain. They were glad a rainbow had been wide enough for two wishes, and that friendship had chosen both paths instead of leaving one friend behind.
