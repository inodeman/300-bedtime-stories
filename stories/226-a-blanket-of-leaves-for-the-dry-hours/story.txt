Femi knelt by the town garden bed with damp soil cooling his fingers. The day was bright and dry, the kind of day that made puddles from yesterday feel like a memory. Stig carried a basket of small plants with deep, careful roots and a sack of fallen leaves saved for mulch. The sky was clear from one roof to the next. Femi patted the earth. "These plants need a plan for sunny days," he said. "Not just a drink for this morning." Stig set the basket in the shade of a broad leaf. "Then we will plant for the dry hours too."

They dug a hole a little deeper than the pot. Femi remembered that roots could follow moisture down, away from the hot top of the soil. He loosened the sides so the roots would not meet a hard wall. Stig held the plant steady while Femi filled around it. They did not press the soil into a brick. They pressed it into a gentle hug. "Air and water both need paths," Femi said. Stig nodded. "Nature likes a path more than a trap."

Next came the leaves. They spread a soft collar of mulch around the stem, not touching it, like a blanket with a hole for a neck. The leaves would keep the sun from baking the soil. They would also slow a later drink so it sank in instead of running off. Femi poured water from a can in a slow circle. The water darkened the mulch and then disappeared in a calm way. "That is a good vanishing," he said. "It went where the roots can find it."

Stig pointed to a sunny corner where the soil looked pale and tired. "What about there?" Femi studied it. That spot drank fast and dried fast. They chose a plant that liked light but still gave it mulch and a nearby flat stone for a small shade at noon. Femi set the stone so it would shadow the roots, not the leaves that needed sun. "We are not hiding the plant from the day," he said. "We are helping it live with the day." The sky stayed wide and friendly overhead.

They made a morning watering mark on a wooden stick. A simple sun drawing meant they would water early, when the air was cool and less would float away. Femi explained it to Stig in a clear voice, and Stig explained it back, to be sure they shared the same plan. A bee visited a nearby bloom and left without being bothered. Femi watched it and felt how busy and peaceful nature could be at once. The garden was not a decoration. It was a neighborhood of roots, leaves, and patient thirst.

Along the path, they noticed a crack in the soil that meant dryness was already visiting. They did not worry it into a problem too big for the afternoon. They filled the crack with loose soil and mulch and gave that place a slow drink. Femi pressed his palm down and felt coolness underneath. "The top is warm, the underneath is a pantry," he said. Stig grinned. "Then we are stocking the pantry." They planted two more flowers and one sturdy herb, each with the same kind collar of leaves.

Before they finished, they set a shallow saucer of water in the deepest shade for small visiting creatures, with a pebble in it so anyone tiny could climb out. Femi liked thinking about more than the plants. Nature was a whole table, and everyone needed a safe sip. They rinsed their hands at the pump and looked back. The bed looked tidy but not stiff. It looked ready. The bright sky would stay bright, and the roots had a cool secret waiting below the leaves.

Femi wrote the plant names on little wooden tags and let Stig place each tag facing the path so neighbors could learn them too. Sharing the names felt like part of caring. A dry day did not have to be a worried day. It could be a planned day, with shade, mulch, and morning water. They stood in the garden's edge of shadow and let the sun warm only their hats. The leaves barely moved. The soil held its drink. That stillness was a good sign.

Femi and Stig walked the edge of the bed one last time and spoke to the plants in ordinary voices, not magic words, just clear care. "Morning drink, leafy collar, stone shade where you need it." Saying the plan aloud helped them remember it and made the garden feel accompanied. They noticed a worm slip back into the cool soil and left it undisturbed. Nature was already at work under their hands. The bright sky would return tomorrow, and the bed would be ready, not worried. They hung the watering can on a shady hook so the next gardener would find both the tool and the idea waiting together.

The lantern by the window glowed softly while Femi set a spare wooden tag beside it and told the evening room about the cool pantry under the mulch. Outside, the sky was clear and gentle, and the garden rested in its blanket of leaves. He felt close to the plants even from home, glad that nature had taught him how to care before the day grew thirsty.
