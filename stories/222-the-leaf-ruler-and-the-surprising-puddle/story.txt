After the short shower, the lane smelled like clean stone and wet leaves. Bjorn carried a coil of string and a piece of chalk. Gracie walked beside him with a flat stick they called their leaf ruler, because a fallen leaf marked the starting end. The rain had already stopped. The sky was pale and bright, with a soft shine at the edges. Puddles sat along the lane like small mirrors. Bjorn's feet wanted to splash at once. He bounced once, then held still. "They look ready," he said. Gracie nodded. "They also look tricky. Let's measure before we decide."

Bjorn knelt by the first puddle. It spread wide across the path and seemed like a little lake. He wanted it to be the deepest. Gracie laid the string along the widest part. Bjorn pinched the place where the water ended and marked that length on the dry stone with chalk. The mark was long. He grinned. "This one wins." Gracie did not argue. She only said that width was one question and depth was another. Patience could ask both. Bjorn liked questions when they felt like a game. He agreed to wait and check before any splash.

They used the leaf ruler for depth. Gracie dipped the stick straight down, slow and careful, until it touched the bottom. The leaf stayed dry. Only a small ring of water showed on the wood. Bjorn blinked. "The big puddle is a shallow plate." He felt a tug of surprise, and then he laughed. His guess had been fast, and the stick had been patient. They wrote a tiny number in chalk, a one, for one finger of water. Bjorn said that if he had jumped, he would have learned less and splashed more. Gracie smiled. "We can still splash a little, after the measuring is done."

The next puddle was narrow, tucked beside a flowerpot. It did not look proud. Bjorn almost walked past it. Then he remembered the first surprise. "We should give this one a fair turn," he said. They measured the width. The chalk mark was short. Then Gracie lowered the stick. The leaf got a damp kiss, and the wet ring climbed higher than before. Bjorn's eyes widened. "The small one is deeper." He sat back on his heels. Patience had let a quiet puddle tell the truth. The lane was full of stories, and the loudest shape was not the whole story.

They kept going, puddle by puddle, never hurrying past a small one. A round puddle by the gate held a picture of the sky. A long puddle near the step was thin as a ribbon and barely wet the stick. Bjorn made a chalk map, circles and lines, with numbers beside them. He counted to ten between each dip so the water could grow still again. Ripples faded. Reflections returned. "Waiting makes the mirror come back," he said. Gracie added that waiting also kept their shoes from doing all the talking. They both giggled, then settled, because the next puddle deserved a calm look.

Bjorn noticed that the sunny side of the lane was already shrinking its puddles. The wide shallow one had pulled in its edges. The chalk mark was now longer than the water. "It changed while we were careful," he said. "If we had rushed, we would have measured a guess." Gracie nodded. "The sun is patient and busy at the same time." They measured the wide puddle again and wrote the new number smaller. Bjorn liked seeing the change. Patience was not empty waiting. It was paying attention while time did its gentle work on the water and the light.

At the end of the lane they chose one fair splash. Bjorn did not leap into the deep puddle by the flowers, because that water rested near the roots. He tapped the shallow edge with his toe. A bright fan of drops rose and fell. Gracie tapped too. The drops caught light and vanished. They looked at their chalk map and felt proud. The deepest puddle had been the modest one. The widest had been a shining trick. Bjorn rolled the string neatly. "I can wait and still have fun," he said. "The fun just arrives with more facts."

They rinsed the stick under the garden pump and set the leaf free on the grass. The sky stayed mild. No new rain hurried them. Bjorn looked back at the chalk numbers and hoped the sun would leave them a little longer. He already planned to measure again later if the puddles remained. Gracie said the deep one might hide under a leaf next time. Bjorn liked that mystery. Patience had given him a reason to return, and the lane would still be there, shining in pieces, ready for careful eyes.

The lantern by the window glowed softly when Bjorn set the chalk and string on the table beside it. He told the quiet room about the puddle that looked big and the puddle that was truly deep. Outside, the lane kept its calm evening shine, and the sky wore a peaceful face. Bjorn rested his chin on his hands and practiced waiting for one more minute, just to feel how kind a careful pause could be.
