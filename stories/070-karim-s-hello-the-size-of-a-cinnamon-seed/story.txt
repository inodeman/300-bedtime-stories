Karim stood just inside the neighborhood bakery with a small card in his pocket and a flutter in his chest. Every Saturday someone said a short hello at the shared table before the bread was passed. It was not a speech. It was a welcome. Today the welcome was his, and his voice felt as small as a cinnamon seed.

Chioma arranged honey jars so they glowed in a line. Pavel set orange peels in a dish and checked the soup pots, which were already full and fragrant. The bread ovens had finished their morning work. Loaves cooled on the rack, crackling softly, more than enough for the market baskets and the long table. Nothing was missing except Karim's hello.

"I know the words," Karim said. "Hello, friends. The bread is ready, and there is plenty. Come sit. But when I imagine the faces, the words hide."

Chioma did not laugh. "Then we will practice where the words can hear you," she said. "Courage can start in a whisper."

They stood by the oven first, where the hum covered his voice like a kind blanket. Karim whispered the hello. Pavel whispered it back, as if the pots were the audience. They tried again near the honey, a little louder. The jars did not frown. Karim's shoulders dropped a notch.

"Now by the window," Pavel said. "The market is noisy. Your hello only needs to reach the table, not the whole street."

Karim looked out. Stalls were bright. People carried picnic baskets. Cinnamon drifted from the open door. He put his hand on the back of a chair. "What if I forget the middle?"

"Then we wait," Chioma said. "A pause is allowed. Patience can hold your courage up."

They walked to the shared table together. Karim did not have to climb onto anything. He only had to stand beside the bread. His heart thumped like a wooden spoon on a pot. He thought of the practice whisper. He thought of the plenty in the baskets. He opened his mouth.

"Hello, friends," he said. The words came out round and clear enough. "The bread is ready, and there is plenty. Come sit." He paused, and the pause did not swallow him. "The soup is warm. The honey is open. There is a place for your hands."

Chioma smiled with her whole face. Pavel lifted a loaf like a quiet cheer. A few neighbors who were already nearby settled onto the bench, drawn by the smell and the hello. Karim's ears felt hot, in a proud way. He sat down before his knees could invent a wobble.

They passed bread. They passed the honey. They did not make Karim talk the whole time. Courage had done its job in those few sentences. Now sharing could take a turn. Karim spread honey and orange peel on a slice and took a bite. The crust was crisp. The middle was cloud-soft. He found he could chat about ordinary things, like how the peel curled, once the hello was behind him and also still inside him.

"You sounded like yourself," Pavel said. "Not like a bell. Like Karim."

"I was afraid I would sound like a squeak," Karim said.

Chioma shook her head. "A squeak can still be a true hello. Yours was better than a squeak. It was a door."

They wrote the welcome on a recipe card, not because it was a recipe for bread, but because it was a recipe for beginning. Hello. Plenty. Come sit. Karim added, If your voice feels small, practice by the oven hum. Then stand by the table. Friends will wait with you.

The market brightened. Baskets slid under the bench. Soup was ladled without rushing. Karim helped pour water so his hands would have a calm job. Each time someone new approached, he did not hide. He nodded, and once he even repeated the short hello, easier the second time, like a peel that bends after it warms.

He kept the card in his pocket when the plates were mostly crumbs and the loaves were still not gone. Plenty had stayed plenty. His courage had not used anything up. It had added a voice to the room.

Karim practiced the hello one last time with no audience except the honey jars, and this time he added a small joke he had not planned. The rolls are warm, and so is the bench. Chioma clapped softly. Pavel said the joke was a window, not a speech, and windows were allowed. They set a cinnamon roll on a plate for each place, plus two extras in the center so a late arrival would not find a bare spot. Karim's hands were steady with the plates. The flutter in his chest had become a warm tick, like an oven doing its job. He looked at the market path and saw baskets and bright sleeves and no reason to hide. When a neighbor paused at the door, Karim did not look down. He lifted his cup in a small welcome and pointed to the open seat. The words were already in the room. His smile finished them.

The lantern by the window glowed softly in front of Karim's place at the shared table. Chioma and Pavel sat on either side of him, honey on their bread and orange in the air. The hello still seemed to hover, small and golden, like cinnamon dust that had found the right spot.
