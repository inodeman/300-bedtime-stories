The playground slide shone like a warm spoon in the sun. Gracie arrived with a basket holding two soft sun hats, both wide enough to make a circle of shade for a face. Tamsin was already by the ladder, squinting and smiling at the same time. Her hair was bright at the edges, and her own hat was still at home on a hook she had forgotten. Gracie saw the squint before Tamsin even asked. "I brought an extra," Gracie said, holding out the paler hat. "Hats are more fun when they are shared."

Tamsin put it on and let out a happy breath. "The sun feels kinder already." They touched the brim like a small roof. Gracie put on the other hat. Now they matched in comfort, not in color. The slide could wait a moment. First they found the big tree at the edge of the playground, where the ground was cool and the light turned green. Gracie set the basket in that shade. "This can be our base," she said. "We can play in the sun, then come back before we feel too warm."

They took turns on the slide, short turns, because sharing the hat did not mean forgetting to share the fun. After two rides, Gracie checked Tamsin's face. "Still good?" Tamsin nodded. "Good, and I want the tree soon." They walked back to the shade without making it a gloomy march. It was part of the game. In the shade they drank water from their bottles and watched other children play under their own hats. Gracie liked how the playground could be sunny and safe at once if people traded time with the tree.

A ball rolled into the shade and stopped by the basket. Gracie picked it up and looked for the owner, a smaller child near the sand. She walked the ball back instead of tossing it into the bright middle. "Shade delivery," she said, and the child laughed and thanked her. Tamsin held their place under the tree so the basket would not feel abandoned. When Gracie returned, Tamsin offered her the cooler side of the bench. "You shared the hat. I can share the best shade." Gracie took it with a grin. Sharing, she thought, moved in a circle.

They made up a simple rule and said it out loud so it felt fair. Two songs in the sun, then one rest in the shade. Gracie hummed the songs so they would not lose count. During the sunny part they played a gentle tag that was really just following shadows of hats on the ground. During the shady part they sat and named which parts of the playground were the coolest. The bench. The tree. The side of the little shed. Tamsin said, "Knowing the shade is like knowing a secret map." Gracie agreed. A map was a kind thing to share.

When a cloud passed over, the whole playground softened. They did not toss the hats aside. "The sun will come back," Gracie said. "The hats can stay ready." The cloud moved on, and the light returned, friendly but strong. They were glad they had not turned the rest into a reason to forget care. Tamsin adjusted Gracie's brim, which had slipped. Gracie straightened Tamsin's, which had gone a little sideways. Helping with a hat was another small share, as useful as handing over a turn on the swing.

Near the end, a friend from the sand asked if the tree had room. Gracie and Tamsin scooted at once. There was enough shade if they shared the edges and kept the basket tidy. They showed the two-song rule, and the new friend liked it. Nobody kept the coolest spot as a prize. The spot traveled. Gracie felt a quiet pride. She had brought two hats, and the day had turned them into more than two comforts. It had turned them into a way of playing that left nobody squinting alone.

They walked home along the side of the lane where the bushes made patches of shade. Gracie carried the basket, and Tamsin carried nothing but the paler hat, which she promised to return tomorrow freshly brushed. "You can borrow it again," Gracie said. "Sharing can last more than one afternoon." Tamsin bumped her brim against Gracie's in a hat hello. The sky was still bright and clear. Their faces felt comfortable, not tight, and the playground behind them glowed without being a problem.

On the shady bench they invented a small thank-you to end the game. Gracie said, "Thank you for the turn in the sun." Tamsin said, "Thank you for the hat and the tree." Then they thanked the breeze that sometimes cooled the slide, even though the breeze could not answer. The ritual made sharing feel complete instead of accidental. Gracie tucked a smooth leaf into the basket as a marker of the day. Tamsin promised to remember the two-song rule even when she brought her own hat. Comfort, they agreed, was something you could pack, pass along, and still have plenty of for yourself.

The lantern by the window glowed softly as Gracie hung both sun hats on the chair beside it. The brims made round shadows on the wall. She liked those shadows. They looked like the tree shade, brought indoors in a small way. Outside, the evening was calm and warm. Gracie was glad a simple extra hat had been enough to share, and that the best games had included a place to rest.
