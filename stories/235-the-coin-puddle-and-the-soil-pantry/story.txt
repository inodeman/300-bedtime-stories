Oleg watched a puddle on the garden stones shrink in the bright sun. A little while earlier, a short shower had left water in many places. Now the stone puddle was a thin coin, while the water in the soil bed still made a dark, cool patch. Bjorn arrived with the leaf ruler and a curious grin. "Are you measuring too?" he asked. Oleg nodded. "I am letting the water teach us where to plant. Nature is giving a lesson with two kinds of disappearing."

They knelt and looked without poking yet. On the stone, the puddle had no place to sink, so the sun lifted it away. In the bed, the soil held the drink between small crumbs of earth. Oleg pressed a finger nearby, not in the muddiest spot, and felt coolness. "Roots can use a pantry like that," he said. Bjorn dipped the leaf ruler at the edge of the soil pool. The wet mark was higher than the stone's coin could ever manage. "The ground is the deeper cup," Bjorn said. Oleg smiled. "Then our plants should live by the cup, not by the plate."

They chose a planting place just beside the dark patch, where the soil was damp but not soupy. Oleg dug a gentle hole. Bjorn dropped in a small flower with careful roots and held it straight. They filled the hole and patted a soft hug of earth around the stem. Then they added a collar of dry leaves, the way the garden plan asked, so tomorrow's sun would not empty the pantry too fast. "We are planting for dry days," Oleg said, "using what a wet hour just showed us." The sky above was already clear and mild.

Bjorn wanted to splash the stone puddle, but he waited and used it as a clock instead. "When the coin is gone," he said, "we will know the sun is working hard, and we will check the mulch." Oleg liked that idea. They sat in the shade of the broad leaves and watched. The coin thinned to a gleam, then to a damp mark, then to dry stone. The soil patch stayed dark. Oleg felt a quiet wonder. Nature did not lecture with words. It lectured with where the water chose to rest.

They gave the new flower a slow drink from a can that had waited in the shade. The water slipped under the leaf collar and stayed. Oleg explained, in a simple way, that morning would be the best time for the next drink, before the day grew warm. Bjorn repeated the plan to prove he had it. Early water. Mulch blanket. Shade for the can. Roots near soil that knows how to hold on. A bee visited a different bloom and left them in peace. The garden sounded like leaves and a far harbor bell, nothing more.

Oleg noticed a crack starting in an older bare spot. He did not feel upset. The crack was another sentence from nature. "This place is asking for a cover," he said. They crumbled soil into the crack, laid leaves over it, and set a flat stone to shadow the roots of a nearby herb. Bjorn measured the stone's shade with his hand. "At noon this will be a small roof," he said. Oleg nodded. "A roof for the ground, and hats for us." They put their sun hats back on as they stepped into the brighter path. Care could be worn and care could be planted.

Before they left the bed, they made a tiny map on a board. A circle for the stone that dried fast. A square for the soil that kept its drink. A star for the new flower. Bjorn added a chalk number, the depth of the wet mark. Oleg added the words dry-day plan. They propped the board where the gardeners would see it. Sharing the lesson made it stronger. The puddle's disappearing act would help more than one afternoon. It would help the next sunny week.

Oleg rinsed his hands and looked back. The stone was dry. The flower stood in a dark, leafy ring. He felt proud in a grounded way. They had not fought the sun. They had learned its habits and given the roots a head start. Bjorn rolled the string he had not even needed, because looking had been enough. "Nature showed us," he said. Oleg answered, "And we listened before the water was gone."

They checked the new flower's leaves for brightness and found them calm, not limp and not scorched. Oleg took that as a sign that the pantry under the mulch was already doing its quiet job. Bjorn measured the shade of the flat stone with the leaf ruler and marked the length in chalk on the board. Numbers and nature sat side by side, which pleased them both. A respectful breeze cooled their hat brims and did not lift the leaf collar away. The lesson felt complete. Water had shown where to plant, and their hands had answered before the dry afternoon could make the soil forget.

The lantern by the window glowed softly as Oleg set the little map board on the sill. The star for the new flower caught the light. Outside, the evening garden rested under a clear, calm sky. He was glad a shrinking puddle had been a teacher, and that planting for dry days could begin with nothing more than patient eyes.
