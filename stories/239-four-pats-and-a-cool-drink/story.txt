Stig set two small trowels on the soil of the town garden bed. Inez set a watering can in the shade and a bundle of leaf mulch beside it. The day was bright and dry, with a clear sky and a breeze too polite to blow the soil away. They had a job that was too handy for one person and just right for two. "We are planting for the sunny hours ahead," Stig said. "If we share the steps, the roots will get a fair start." Inez nodded. "Teamwork can be a pattern. You dig, I place, we both pat, and nobody rushes the drink."

They marked three spots with pebbles. Stig loosened the first hole, breaking clumps so the earth would be a path and not a wall. Inez waited with the plant instead of digging a second hole too soon. Waiting for the team was part of the team. When the hole was ready, she set the roots in and held the stem straight. Stig filled soil in small scoops. Then they both patted the surface with open hands, counting four gentle pats so they would not pack it hard. "Even the pats are shared," Inez said. Stig grinned. "Four for you in spirit, four for me in spirit, and the plant gets them all."

The second hole tested them. Stig hit a stubborn crumb and wanted to jab. Inez offered a slower twist of the trowel. He tried her way, and the crumb loosened without a mess. "Your move was better," he said. "We will use it on the third." Inez felt the praise land lightly, not proudly. She then needed his stronger scoop to lift a stone aside. "Your move now," she said. The stone rolled to the path, useful later as a little shade roof. They were trading strengths the way people trade chalk, without keeping score.

Mulch came next, and it wanted four hands. Inez lifted the bundle. Stig spread the leaves in a collar that did not touch the stems. When the collar grew too thick on one side, Inez smoothed it. When it grew thin, Stig added more. They stepped back often, because a team needs a look from a distance, not only busy fingers. The bed began to look cozy, as if the soil had been given a light blanket for dry days. The watering can still rested in the shade, cool and ready, not dumped in a hurry.

They watered as a pair. Stig tipped the can. Inez pointed to the circle around each plant so the drink would not run off to the stones. The water went in, dark and quiet. A third seedling, planted with the same pattern, received the same care. No plant got the leftover splash and nothing more. Fairness was a kind of teamwork too. Inez said so, and Stig agreed. "If one of us forgets a step, the other can remember out loud," he said. "That is not bossing. That is keeping the pattern whole."

They made a marker together. Stig cut a flat stick. Inez drew a simple sun and a leaf on it, a reminder of morning water and mulch. They pressed it into the soil at the corner. Then they walked the bed once more, checking pats, collars, and the stone shade. A small weed had tried to join the party. Stig held the soil while Inez lifted the weed out gently and set it in the compost pail, because even that tiny chore was better with two careful hands. The garden smelled like water and leaves. The sky stayed mild and wide.

Before they washed up, they taught the pattern to each other from memory. Stig said the steps. Inez said them backward, just for fun, and they laughed when backward made the drink arrive before the hole. "Forward is wiser," Inez said. They bumped trowel handles like a toast. Other afternoons might bring kites or fog or a rainbow, but this afternoon's teamwork would stay in the ground, working quietly whenever the sun grew strong. That thought made Stig feel useful beyond the hour. Inez felt the same. A shared job kept helping after the workers went home.

They rinsed the tools and set them under the bench where the next gardeners would find them easily. The plants stood in their leafy rings, small and ready. Stig and Inez did not need to wait and watch every minute. Trusting the plan was the last step of the team. They put on sun hats for the bright path and walked out of the garden side by side, already able to tell the story of who did what, and how both mattered. They looked once more at the pebble markers and saw that none had been forgotten, which made the pattern feel trustworthy in their hands and in the ground.

The lantern by the window glowed softly as Stig and Inez described the four pats and the cool drink to the quiet evening room. Outside, the sky was clear and restful. Their teamwork had gone into the soil, where it would keep being kind on dry days, even while the lantern kept its own steady glow at home.
