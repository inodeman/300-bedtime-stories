The recipe cards on the bakery counter were full of careful words and almost no pictures. Lewis read one aloud. Mix. Rest. Fold honey. Add orange peel. Sprinkle cinnamon. Bake. Share. Chioma listened and lost the middle, not because she was careless, but because the words looked like a fence without gates. Otto turned the card around as if a drawing might be hiding. The back was blank.

"I can bake if someone reads," Chioma said. "When I read alone, the steps trade places."

Lewis tapped his pencil. "Then the card can grow pictures. Words for the word-readers. Drawings for the eye-readers. Both can be true."

They spread five cards on the shared table. The Saturday light helped. Honey jars stood nearby like shiny models. A bowl of orange peels curled at the edge. Cinnamon sticks lay in a tin. The bread ovens hummed, and the soup pots filled the room with a soft second song. Nothing they needed was scarce. What was scarce was a picture.

Otto drew a small honey jar with a ribbon pouring out, right in the margin beside the honey step. His jar looked a bit like a boot at first. He laughed and rounded it. "Creativity includes a second try," he said.

Chioma drew three orange curls that looked like smiles. Lewis drew a cinnamon stick and tiny dots falling from it. Then they made a loaf, a simple oval with a wavy steam line. For the share step, they drew the long table and three cups. The drawings were small enough to leave the words alone and clear enough to lead a finger down the page.

They tested the cards with their hands, not only their eyes. Chioma picked a card and followed the pictures without anyone reading. She scooped honey when she saw the jar. She waited when she saw a little clock they had added, a circle with a patient face, not a rush. She tucked peels where the smiles were. Lewis watched and did not jump in. Otto only pointed if she asked. Teamwork here was quiet.

The dough became a real loaf. Into the oven it went. While it baked, they repaired a wobbly drawing of the oven so it would not look like a door to nowhere. Lewis added the word rest under the clock. Chioma added the word share under the table. Pictures and words held hands.

When the loaf came out, the crust was golden and the peel had made bright freckles. They sliced it at the shared table and tasted. The crumb matched the card. Chioma's eyes shone. "I did not lose the middle," she said. "The smiles and the jar kept me on the path."

Otto nodded. "And I did not lose the words. Your labels kept my boots from becoming jars forever."

They made more cards for the market stall, enough for a basket, not so many that the drawing became sloppy. Patience kept the lines kind. One card showed the soup pot with a peel going in early and another card showed the peel going in late, a tiny nod to a question they had heard that morning. Another card showed two picnic baskets and one big braid, because sharing needed a picture too.

At the stall, they set the cards in a box with pencils so neighbors could take one and even add a doodle of their own if they wished. The bakery still had the first set, propped by the ovens. Plenty of cards, plenty of bread, plenty of ways to understand.

Lewis watched a shopper trace the honey jar with a finger and smile. "The margin was empty," he said. "Empty was not the same as finished."

Chioma stacked the extra cards in a neat brick. "A picture is a kindness to the next hands."

Otto blew a stray cinnamon dot off a card and then sneezed a tiny sneeze that made them all grin. The sneeze did not smudge the share table drawing. They called that good luck and also good planning, because the ink was already dry.

The market brightened. Baskets swung. Inside, the new loaf sat beside the older words, now friendlier. The ovens ticked as they cooled. The soup pots stayed full for later. On the table, the cards looked like little windows.

Chioma traced every picture on the finished card with a clean finger, naming each one in a whisper. Jar. Clock. Smiles. Stick. Loaf. Table. The whisper helped the memory settle. Lewis added a tiny picnic basket in the corner for the step that said carry some to the stall, because carrying was part of sharing too. Otto shaded the honey ribbon so it looked wet. They held the card at arm's length the way a baker checks a loaf, and the path down the page was easy to see. No step hid behind another. The real loaf on the table matched the drawn loaf well enough to make them proud. Cinnamon dotted the margin like the dots Lewis had drawn, a rhyme between paper and crumb. They left the pencil in a cup so the next helper could add a picture if a new step ever appeared.

The lantern by the window glowed softly across the drawings of jars, peels, and the long table. Lewis, Chioma, and Otto leaned in, flour on their sleeves and pencils behind their ears. The recipe had not changed its kindness. It had simply learned another language, and the shared morning could read both.
