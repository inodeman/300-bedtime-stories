Eero knelt at the rim of the plaza fountain, where the water was shallow and clear enough to show the pale tiles. In the middle, on a low stone, sat a turtle carved with a smile, not a real animal, just a friendly shape the town liked. Eero had made a boat from a cork, a twig mast, and a green leaf sail. He set it down with hope. The boat spun in a tight circle, once, twice, and never aimed at the turtle. "It only dances," he said. "I wanted it to travel."

The fountain minder, a grown-up with rolled sleeves, watched without laughing at him. "The leaf is a good idea," the minder said. "It is also a big idea for such a small cork. A wide sail catches more push than the boat can steer." Eero pinched his lip. He liked the leaf big. It looked like a flag. "If I tear it off, the boat is only a cork," he said. "Do not tear," said the minder. "Try a change you can undo. Fold, do not rip. And give the cork a little steady weight so it sits, not skitters."

Eero lifted the boat. The leaf was damp at one edge and dry at the other, which made the spin worse. He folded the leaf in half so it became a smaller sail, still green, still pretty, and he tied it again with a thread. From the fountain rim he chose a white pebble no bigger than a pea, rinsed and smooth. "May this ride as a passenger?" he asked. "A light passenger, yes," said the minder. Eero set the pebble in a dip in the cork. The boat sat lower and calmer in his hand, like a person who had decided not to fidget.

He placed it on the water near the rim, nose toward the turtle. A soft push of his finger, no flick, sent it going. The boat did not spin. It wobbled, thought about spinning, and then went forward in a slow line. Eero held his breath and then let it out so he would not blow the sail by accident. "It is traveling," he whispered. The leaf, smaller now, did a modest job. The pebble kept the cork from tipping into a dance. The smiling turtle waited as if it had time, which it did.

The boat reached the stone and bumped it gently, a tap no louder than a raindrop. Eero grinned. "The big sail was the problem, not the whole boat." The minder nodded. "You changed one part and the idea worked. That is creativity. Not a brand-new everything. A wiser something." Eero walked around the fountain to meet his boat, shoes quiet on the tiles. He lifted it carefully so the leaf would not crush. Water beads shone on the cork like tiny glass.

He tried the crossing once more, to see if it had been luck. The boat went straight again, a little to the left, still true enough to find the turtle. Eero laughed. "Second time is a real time," he said. He thought about the paper flags on the bridge and how a too-big flag might twist a string. Making was full of sizes. The right size was a kind of kindness to the thing you made. The clock tower chimed, and the water rings from the boat met the rings from the fountain's small jet and got along.

A younger child asked, "Will you show me the fold?" Eero sat on the dry rim and showed the half-leaf, the thread, the pea pebble. "You can unfold it if you want the dance back," he said. "Today I wanted the trip." The child nodded, serious and pleased. They sent the boat once more and watched it choose the turtle without being chased. No sticks stirred the water. The fountain stayed a calm place.

Eero dried his fingers on his shirt and set the boat on a cloth to rest. The leaf sail, folded, looked like a neat green ear. He felt proud in a quiet way. The boat had not needed to be thrown away or scolded. It had needed a smaller sail and a small weight. Ideas, he decided, could be repaired by noticing which part was doing too much.

He walked once around the fountain with the boat in the cloth, showing his grown-up the folded sail and the pea pebble without dripping on anyone's shoes. "The turtle did not have to chase it," he said. "The boat learned the way." His grown-up asked which part he would change if he made another, and Eero said he would start with a small sail instead of learning the hard way. Then he grinned. "But I am glad I learned it. The spin was funny before it was frustrating." They agreed the funny part could stay a story, while the boat itself stayed a traveler.

The lantern by the window glowed softly in the fountain minder's little window at the plaza edge, early evening light mixing with the lamp. Eero looked from that glow to the smiling turtle. Changing the leaf, not the whole boat, had let his idea finish its trip. He carried the cork boat home beside his grown-up, already wondering what else a careful fold could fix.
