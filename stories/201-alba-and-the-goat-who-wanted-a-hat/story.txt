Alba woke when the meadow light reached her pillow. Her cream sun hat hung on the porch peg, wide and soft, with a ribbon that never pinched. She tied it under her chin and stepped outside. Dew cooled her toes. Near the open gate, Bramble the goat lifted her head and gave a friendly bleat that sounded like a small bell made of breath.

Bramble was gentle. She had careful feet, warm brown eyes, and a habit of waiting before she came closer. She liked clover, shade, and quiet company. Today she did not look at the clover first. She looked at Alba's hat. She looked again. She took one polite step and tilted her ears toward the brim, as if the hat were a question she hoped someone would answer.

Alba stood still so Bramble would not feel rushed. "Do you want a hat too?" she asked. Bramble blinked slowly, which was her calm yes. She sniffed the ribbon. The ribbon tickled, and Bramble made a tiny sound that was almost a laugh. Alba smiled. A hat for a goat could not be a tight people hat. It had to be light. It had to rest, not grab. It had to come off with one easy shake if Bramble wanted the wind in her ears.

Alba opened the cloth basket on the porch. Inside were leftover squares from mending day, pale yellow and leaf green, clean and soft. "We can make something," she told Bramble. Bramble followed only as far as the step and waited, because the porch was a people place and she liked to be invited. The grown-up of the house came out with a needle already threaded. "A kind idea," the grown-up said. "We will sew it so it shades her eyes and never squeezes."

They spread the squares on the wooden table. Alba chose yellow for the top and green for the brim. She held the edges while the grown-up stitched wide, lazy seams. They did not hurry. Far out in the clover a bee hummed, and they let the sound stay far away. Alba tried the little hat on her own hand first. It weighed about as much as a folded napkin. The cotton tie was long enough for a bow that could slip free. "Only if you want it," Alba said, and held it out on open palms.

Bramble sniffed the brim. She lowered her head a little, which meant yes. Alba set the hat between Bramble's ears. It sat lightly. The green brim made a small pool of shade. Bramble stood very still, listening to the new feeling, then took three proud steps along the fence. She was not led. She was not pulled. She simply walked, pleased and free, and the grass nodded around her hooves.

A warm wind crossed the meadow. The hat shifted. Bramble gave one small shake, and the bow slipped loose, just as they had planned. The hat landed softly in the grass. Bramble nosed it once and looked at Alba, as if the game were good. Alba laughed and tied the bow again, looser than before. "You can wear it when the sun feels bright," she said. "You can set it down whenever you wish." They walked the path side by side. Alba wore cream. Bramble wore yellow and green. Ducks murmured at the far pond. The pony swished a tail in the shade and did not mind them passing.

They met the chickens beside a dry sunny patch, where dust puffed like cinnamon when a wing fluttered just for fun. The chickens did not want hats. Their feathers already made a fine cover. Alba felt glad she had not guessed that every animal wanted the same present. She told the nearest hen, "Bramble asked with her eyes, so we answered Bramble." The hen gave a comfortable cluck and went on dusting. Bramble stood a little apart, brim slightly tilted, looking proud but not showy.

Hawa came from the herb pots and asked if the brim might be touched. Alba said yes, and Hawa used one careful finger. Then Alba showed the loose bow. "If Bramble wants the wind, the hat leaves," she said. They tried one practice shake together. The bow opened. The hat settled in the grass. Bramble nosed it and waited, which meant she would like it on again. Alba tied it more gently than before. Choosing, she decided, was the kindest stitch in the whole afternoon, better than any perfect bow.

Before they went in, Alba set a shallow pan of water near the peg so Bramble could drink after her proud walk. The goat took a few tidy sips and left the rest, which was allowed. Alba told the grown-up every step of the bow plan, including the practice shake that had worked. "Kindness fits when a friend can say yes and also set the gift down," she said. The grown-up agreed and put the needle away in its tin, job finished, no extra stitches needed.

The lantern by the window glowed softly when they came back, because the grown-up liked to light it early. Alba hung the little hat on a low peg Bramble could reach or leave. Bramble touched it with her nose, then went to chew clover by the open gate. Alba waved. Kindness, she decided, can be as simple as a hat a friend may choose. The yellow brim dried in the warm air, ready for the next bright hour and never, ever tight.
