Ioanna had waved at Pippin the pony many times. She had watched his steady walk from the gate. She had never walked beside him. The idea felt large, even though Pippin himself was known for being gentle. His coat was the color of toast. His eyes were quiet. Still, Ioanna's knees sent a small message that said this is new. She stood at the gate and told the truth. "I want to try, and I also feel wiggly inside."

The grown-up did not laugh at the wiggles. "That mix is allowed," the grown-up said. "Courage often shows up wearing ordinary shoes. I will walk on Pippin's other side the whole time. We will go to the first oak and back. If you want to stop, we stop. Pippin likes a clear plan." Ioanna breathed out. A short walk had edges she could see. Edges made the new thing friendly.

They greeted Pippin at his open shelter, where he could come and go to the pasture as he pleased. The grown-up offered a hand to sniff, and Ioanna copied, palm flat, no grabbing. Pippin's breath was warm and grassy. He blinked a slow blink. The grown-up clipped a soft lead, not to drag him, but to walk together in a way everyone understood. "You may walk by my shoulder," the grown-up told Ioanna. "You do not need to hold the lead today. Being beside him is the brave part."

The first steps were the loudest inside Ioanna's ears. Gravel talked. Pippin's hoof sounds were bigger than her shoes. She watched the grown-up's calm pace and matched it. When her heart hurried, her feet did not. She said, "I am walking with you, Pippin," because a voice can be a handrail. Pippin's ear swiveled toward her and then forward again. He did not jump. He did not rush. He seemed to think the lane was exactly the right size for the morning.

Halfway to the oak, a leaf skated across the path. Ioanna paused. The grown-up paused. Pippin paused, looked, and continued when the leaf had finished being interesting. Nobody called it a problem. Ioanna felt her wiggles shrink into something more like attention. She noticed the smell of warm pony, the shine on his shoulder, the way he chose each step. Courage, she realized, was not a shout. It was staying with a good plan while new sounds happened.

At the oak they stopped, which was the promise. Ioanna looked back and was surprised by how far a gentle distance could be. "We did it," she said, and her smile used her whole face carefully, so she would not startle anyone with a sudden cheer. She did not ask to go farther. Stopping at the oak made the courage feel trustworthy. The grown-up offered her the chance to walk on the outside for the return, still close, still not holding the lead. Ioanna said yes. The yes was easier than the first yes, and that felt like growing.

On the way back she told Pippin about the chickens' song, using Hawa's slow tune without expecting him to sing. He flicked an ear at the last note. Near the shelter she thanked him and stepped aside so the grown-up could slip the lead off. Pippin walked into his shade, free to rest or to graze. He chose a mouthful of grass. Ioanna's knees had a new message now. It said we can do a short brave thing.

She washed her hands at the pump and drank cool water. The walk stayed in her legs in a pleasant way. She drew an oak, a pony, and a small person between the gate and the tree, with the grown-up drawn as a tall kind line.

Ioanna told Kira about the oak before the feeling could fade into an ordinary shrug. "I was wiggly, and I still went, and we stopped where we promised," she said. Kira nodded as if that were a recipe worth keeping. They looked at Pippin from the gate, not calling him over, letting him keep the rest of his day. Ioanna's hand remembered the warm grassy breath and did not need to grab a mane to prove she had been brave. The short walk sat in her memory like a smooth stone in a pocket, small enough to carry and solid enough to trust the next time a new path asked for her yes.

The grown-up hung the soft lead on its hook and said the hook was the end of the brave part, not a prize shelf. Ioanna liked that. She stretched her fingers, still a little tingly from paying attention, and drank another sip of water. "Same oak tomorrow, if Pippin is in the mood and you are with me," she said. The grown-up said yes. Courage with a repeatable plan felt sturdier than courage with a bigger dare. Ioanna nodded at the pasture, a small nod, and Pippin did not need to answer for the promise to be good.

The lantern by the window glowed softly over the drawing. Ioanna pinned it low, where she could see it in the morning and remember the oak. Tomorrow she might walk the same path again, not a longer one. Courage did not need to become a bigger dare. It only needed to stay honest, accompanied, and kind.
