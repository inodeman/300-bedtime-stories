The kitten was a gray curl on the sunny rug, no bigger than a mitten, with ears that made tiny pink tents. Tova held her breath so she would not squeak. Wynn's hands hovered, full of the wish to scoop and snuggle at once. The room smelled like warm dust and the clean towel Mama had folded into a basket. Afternoon light lay in stripes. The kitten opened one eye, saw two large children, and poured herself under the sofa in a silent slide. Only the tip of a tail remained, twitching once, then going still.

"We were too much," Tova whispered. Her whisper still felt big. Wynn sat all the way down on the floor so he would be lower and calmer. "Mama said a new kitten is learning the house," he said. "We can learn her, too. Learning is slow." The sofa made a small fabric sigh as the kitten settled farther back. They could hear a faint purr that was not a purr yet, more like a question. Tova's knees wanted to bounce. She pressed her palms flat on the rug until the bounce moved into the floor instead of into the air.

They decided to build a hello that did not chase. A cardboard box from the grocery corner became a nook after Mama tore the tape with her own hands so the children would not meet a sticky edge. Tova lined it with the soft towel. It smelled like soap and sunshine. Wynn cut nothing. He tied a short ribbon to a pencil, with Mama checking the knot, and let the ribbon rest on the rug like a sleepy string. "Not a pounce toy yet," he said. "A later toy. First we wait." The ribbon was blue and a little shiny. It did not jump around. That was the point.

They sat against the wall, far enough to be polite. Tova counted her breaths the way Grandpa had shown her on windy days. One breath in, smelling the towel. One breath out, watching the light move. Wynn told a story in a voice like a turning page, about a mitten that went looking for a warm windowsill. He did not add drums or shouts. The clock in the hall clicked. Somewhere a cup settled on a saucer. The house stayed calm on purpose. Patience, Tova thought, was a kind of quiet you make with your body.

A gray ear appeared at the sofa's edge. Then a nose, pink and serious. The kitten sniffed the air, which held no sudden hands. She took three small steps onto the rug. Her paws made no sound at all. Tova felt a laugh rise and held it gently so it would not leap out. Wynn's fingers stayed on his own knees. The kitten reached the ribbon, touched it with one paw, and watched it barely move. She seemed to approve of toys that minded their manners. She stepped into the towel box, turned once, and became a curl again, this time where they could see her.

"Hello," Tova said, no louder than the clock. The kitten's ear flicked. She did not run. Wynn slid a saucer of water a little closer, the way Mama had said, from the side, not from above. The saucer whispered on the floor. The kitten watched the water tremble and then looked at the children as if checking that the room would stay this size. It would. They had decided. Tova's legs tingled from sitting still, and she welcomed the tingle. It meant she had waited long enough for something small and good.

They did not pick her up. Not today. Mama said laps were for later, when the kitten did the choosing. Instead they named the feeling, not the kitten, because the family was still deciding on a name. "This feeling is patience," Wynn said. Tova nodded. "It feels like a soft towel." They stayed until the stripe of sun moved off the box and the kitten's breathing went slow and even. A tiny snore, hardly a sound, made them both smile into their sleeves. The ribbon lay still. The house smelled like soap and a little like warm fur.

When they finally stood, they stood in slow motion, knees unfolding, voices left behind on the rug. Tova placed a cardboard lip at the box door so it would feel cozy, not closed. Wynn coiled the extra ribbon into the cup by the plant, away from curious paws. They looked back once. The gray curl had one paw over her nose. The room held her carefully. They could come again after supper, still quiet, still glad.

The lantern by the window glowed softly, and the kitten's box sat inside that gentle circle of light. Tova and Wynn watched from the doorway, shoulders touching, not rushing the moment. The ribbon did not twitch. The small gray back rose and fell, a quiet tide. Patience had made a place, and the place was warm, and nobody needed to be loud to say welcome. Even the clock seemed to tick more kindly, as if the whole house had agreed to wait.
