On the sunny porch, Elif spread smooth shells in a row. Miren set down wooden beads, a short stick, and a bundle of soft string. They were making a wind chime that would greet the breeze with a friendly sound. The sky above the roof was bright and easy, with no hurry in it. Elif held two shells to her ear. "I want our porch to sound like a hello," she said. Miren nodded. "A hello, not a shout. We can build that together."

They tied the stick so it hung from the porch beam. Elif chose the longest shell for the middle. Miren chose a bead that looked like a little moon. At first they tied every piece close together because they were excited to hear something right away. The breeze came, respectful and light, and the shells clacked in a crowd. The sound was busy and a bit sharp. Elif wrinkled her nose. "That is more bump than song." Miren did not frown at the work. "Then we listen and we change it. Friends can fix a song."

They untied the knots without blaming the shells or each other. Elif suggested more space between each piece. Miren suggested longer strings on one side and shorter strings on the other, so the notes would fall at different times. They tried Elif's idea first, all the way, before mixing in Miren's. That was their rule for the afternoon. Each friend would get a full turn. When the shells hung farther apart, the breeze could walk between them. The next chime was softer, like two neighbors waving instead of bumping elbows.

Miren then added the moon bead on a middle string. It tapped a shell only when the breeze grew a little braver. The sound was a small tock, then a rest, then a bright click. Elif laughed. "Your bead is the polite drum." She added one tiny shell high up, her own new idea, and asked Miren before she tied the final knot. "Does this crowd your moon?" Miren listened through a whole breath of wind. "No. It answers it." They both looked pleased. Friendship, they could feel, was the space they left for each other's notes.

They sat on the porch floor and closed their eyes for a test. Elif heard the long shell. Miren heard the bead. Then they named the pattern together. Click, rest, tock, rest, a whisper of shells. A bird in the yard seemed to pause, as if it enjoyed the song too. Elif opened her eyes. "I like your part as much as mine," she said. Miren smiled. "I like the way you waited to tie the knot. Waiting made room." They bumped shoulders gently and let the next breeze take a solo.

A stronger puff arrived, still kind, and the chime spoke faster. For a moment Elif wanted to grab the stick and stop it. She looked at Miren instead. "Is it too much?" Miren shook her head. "It is the breeze telling a quicker story. Our job is to make sure nothing tangles." They checked the strings. Nothing was knotted. The chime settled again into its hello. Elif felt proud that she had asked instead of deciding alone. A porch song belonged to both builders and to the air.

They made a tiny plan for care. On very quiet days they would leave the chime up so even a small breeze could practice. If rain came, they would bring the shells inside to stay smooth and dry. Elif wrote the plan on a paper square and Miren drew a shell beside the words. They taped it inside the door, low enough for both of them to read. "We made a promise to the song," Miren said. Elif agreed. Promises were part of friendship, especially the gentle kind that kept a shared idea safe.

When they stepped back into the yard, the porch looked the same and also brand new. The stick turned a little. The shells greeted them even as they left. Elif waved at the chime as if it were a third friend. Miren waved too. "Tomorrow we can listen again and see if the song wants one more bead," she said. Elif liked that tomorrow was included. Friendship was not one afternoon. It was the next hello, and the next, kept light enough to swing in a respectful breeze.

Before suppertime they gave the chime a name that belonged to both of them. Elif offered "Porch Hello." Miren offered "Soft Steps." They put the names together and kept both, because friendship did not need a winner on a sunny porch. Each time the breeze visited, they whispered the double name and smiled when the shells answered. A neighbor walking past lifted a hand to the pleasant sound and did not need to stop long. Elif and Miren waved back. Their homemade song had room for the street as well as for the two makers who had listened so carefully to each other.

The lantern by the window glowed softly as Elif and Miren laid the extra shells in a bowl beside it. The porch chime spoke once from outside, a far friendly click. They listened and smiled at the same time. The evening sky was clear and mild. In the lantern light their paper plan looked important and warm, a reminder that friendship sounds sweetest when everyone has room to ring.
