Kesia pressed her nose to the window while a short shower tapped the glass. The drops raced in silver lines. Rina sat in the window seat with two cups of warm milk and a folded blanket. Beyond the roofs, the sun was already trying to return, but the rainbow Kesia wanted was not there yet. "I wish it would hurry," Kesia said. "I know rainbows come after rain, so why is the sky still only gray and bright at the edges?" Rina patted the cushion. "Because meeting takes a moment. Sun and rain have to find each other. Patience is how we let them."

Kesia sat, though her knees bounced. She watched a drop reach the bottom of the pane and join a tiny river. Rina did not tell her to stop wanting the rainbow. Wanting was fine. The skill was waiting without turning the wait into a grumble. "Let's give the shower a thank-you while it finishes," Rina said. They named three good things about the rain. It washed the leaves. It filled the garden's cool soil. It made the lane smell like clean stones. Kesia's bounce slowed. Gratitude gave her something to do with the time.

The tapping grew lighter. Kesia leaned forward. "Now?" she asked. The sky over the harbor was still a soft pearl, and the sun was a bright smear, not a full face. Rina shook her head kindly. "Not yet. If we stare so hard we get frustrated, we might miss the first pale hint. Let's look, then sip, then look again." They made that into a pattern. Look. Sip. Look. The milk was sweet. The blanket was warm. The shower began to leave gaps between its drops, like words with rests between them.

Kesia noticed the light on the wet roof turning from gray to honey. She clutched Rina's sleeve, then let go and put her hands in her lap on purpose. "I can wait with my hands quiet," she said. Rina smiled. "That is a strong kind of waiting." A bird shook water from a branch and flew to a higher twig, in no rush. Kesia copied the bird's calm in her shoulders. She counted ten slow breaths. At breath ten, a faint color touched the sky beyond the garden, so shy she was not sure.

"I think I see a hint of a bow," she whispered, careful not to rush the moment, though she knew colors were not that timid. Rina looked. "I see it too. We waited long enough for it to introduce itself." They did not shout for it to grow. They stayed with the pattern. Look. Sip. Look. The red appeared, then a warm gold, then a green that seemed to hum. Kesia's eyes shone. "Patience did not make the rainbow," she said. "But patience kept me here to meet it." Rina nodded. "That is exactly its job."

They opened the window a little now that the shower was only a memory of drops. The air smelled fresh. The rainbow stood over the far trees, complete and gentle. Kesia wanted to run outside at once. She checked the step first. It was wet, but the shower was over, and the sky over the town was bright. Rina said they could step onto the porch and no farther until their shoes were ready. Kesia agreed. Even with the prize in the sky, patience still had a small task, keeping them steady on a slippery stone.

On the porch they shared the view in turns. Kesia described the top band. Rina described where the bow seemed to lean toward the harbor. Then they switched, and each friend found a detail the other had missed, a paler stripe, a reflection in a puddle. Kesia laughed. "Waiting made more color, not less." The rainbow held for a lovely while, then thinned in a peaceful way. Kesia did not stamp her foot. "It came, and it left kindly," she said. "I can let a good thing end."

Inside again, they dried the windowsill and set the cups in the sink. Kesia looked back once and saw only blue. She felt full rather than robbed. The waiting had been a room she knew how to sit in now. Rina folded the blanket. "Next shower, we will remember the pattern," she said. Kesia repeated it. Look. Sip. Look. The words felt like a little song for patient eyes.

Back in the window seat they made a patience clock from paper, a circle divided into look, sip, and look again. Kesia colored the slices with soft colors from the rainbow they had finally met. Rina added tiny rain marks on the rim, a thank-you to the shower for leaving at the right time. The clock was not for hurrying. It was for remembering how to wait. Kesia set it on the cushion where her bouncing knees had been. Next time the glass filled with drops, the clock would be there, calm as a friend, ready to keep her company until color gathered in the sky.

The lantern by the window glowed softly where the rainbow had been in the glass a short time before. Kesia watched the flame-colored light and thought of red and gold. The evening outside was calm and clear. She was glad she had not rushed the sky, because patience had saved her a seat at the exact moment the colors arrived.
