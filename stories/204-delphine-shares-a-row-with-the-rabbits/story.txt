Delphine loved the garden border, where clover, loose lettuce, and soft herbs met the meadow. She had watered it with a small can every morning. She had a plan for a big green salad. Then she saw three rabbits in the clover, noses busy, ears tall, completely at home. They were not in a hurry. They were not hiding. They had found a good breakfast in the row she had been saving in her mind.

Delphine's first feeling was a pinch. "That is my lettuce," she whispered. The largest rabbit paused, looked at her with a round dark eye, and went back to nibbling. No one was rude. They were simply hungry in a rabbit way. Delphine sat on the path so she would not loom over them. She watched a leaf tremble and then go still. She thought about the bowl waiting in the kitchen, and she thought about the open meadow, which had food but not this tender row.

The grown-up came with a mug of water and sat too. "They found the sweet part," the grown-up said. "What do you want to do?" Delphine did not want to shout. She did not want to wave her arms. She also did not want to give away every leaf. She picked two short sticks from the kindling tin. "A sharing plan," she said. "This stick marks the row for rabbits. This stick marks the row for our salad. I will tell them with my calm body, and I will pick only on my side."

She pressed the sticks into the soil where the rows already divided themselves. The rabbit side had clover and the outer lettuce. The home side had the inner lettuce and the herbs. Delphine spoke softly. "This part is for you. This part is for us." The rabbits did not understand sticks the way people do, but they understood space. Delphine stayed on her path. She did not reach across. When the smallest rabbit hopped a little too far, Delphine did not lunge. She gently set a flat basket as a low wall on her own row, a hint rather than a fence that closed anyone in. The rabbit sniffed the basket, lost interest, and returned to clover.

Delphine picked her salad slowly. She took outer leaves and left the hearts to grow. She hummed so the rabbits would know where she was. Sharing, she noticed, was not only handing something over. It was deciding ahead of time, then keeping the decision with quiet manners. The largest rabbit washed its face with both paws. The middle rabbit stretched. They looked full in a comfortable way, not frantic. Delphine's bowl looked full too.

She brought the salad to the outdoor table and rinsed it. A few clover heads had come along by accident. She carried those back and set them on the rabbit side. "Extra, because the plan can be kind," she said. Then she ate her share with bread that was fresh and meant for people. She did not toss crumbs toward the border. The rabbits had plants. People had the bowl. The difference felt peaceful.

In the afternoon she checked the sticks. Both rows still had green in them. The rabbits rested under the hedge, easy to see and not worried. Delphine waved with two fingers. One ear twitched, which she decided to count as a wave back.

Delphine returned after lunch with the watering can and did one side at a time. She watered the rabbit row first so she would not forget the part that was no longer only hers. The clover leaves held drops like tiny spoons. A young rabbit crept out, saw the drops, and lapped one from a leaf without coming near her shoes. Delphine stayed on her heels. "The plan still stands," she said. She watered her own lettuce next, humming the same hum, so the garden heard one calm person and not a sudden storm of hands.

Later she told Gideon about the sticks. Gideon started to suggest something taller between the rows, then stopped and looked again. "If they are not spoiling the plants you kept, the sticks are enough," Gideon said. Delphine agreed. Sharing did not need a wall. It needed a promise she could see each morning. She touched each stick once, the way you touch a bookmark, and left the rabbits to their shade. The salad bowl inside was empty and rinsed, which felt like a finished kindness rather than a loss.

At dusk Delphine walked the border once more without the basket. Both rows still held green. The rabbits were shadows under the hedge, resting, not worried, not pushed. She whispered good night and did not add a third stick. A plan can be trusted when you stop improving it every hour. Inside, she washed the lettuce bowl and set it upside down, ready for another fair day.

The lantern by the window glowed softly while Delphine drew her plan on paper: a row of clover dots, a row of lettuce cups, and three round rabbits between them. She pinned the drawing inside the cupboard door. Tomorrow she would water both rows. Sharing would still be there in the soil, waiting, clear as two little sticks and gentle as a bowl set down instead of waving arms.
