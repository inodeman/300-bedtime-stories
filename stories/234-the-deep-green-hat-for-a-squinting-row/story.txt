Nia swung a basket of sun hats as she walked the garden path on a bright, dry day. The sky was clear and generous with light. Femi knelt by a row of young plants, and he was squinting so hard his nose wrinkled. His own hat sat on a far bench, forgotten beside a watering can that baked in the sun. Nia saw all of that in one glance. Kindness, she thought, starts when you notice a squint and a thirsty can. "Femi," she called, "the day is strong. I brought shade you can wear."

She held out a deep green hat. Femi took it with a grateful sound that was almost a laugh. "I was trying to finish this row," he said. "The sun finished my comfort first." Nia tied the brim string loosely so it would not bother him. Then she picked up the hot watering can with the cloth from her basket and carried it into the tree shade. "Water stays cooler here," she said. "And so do gardeners." Femi followed, rolling his shoulders as the shade reached him. His squint eased. His smile returned to its usual size.

They did not rush back into the brightest patch. Nia asked what the plants needed, and Femi explained the mulch and the morning drink he and Stig had planned. Nia listened all the way through. Listening was part of the kindness, so her help would fit the garden instead of rearranging it by accident. Then she offered a simple trade. She would move the tools into the shade and hand Femi the small plants, and he could stay under the brim and the branch while he worked. "You do not have to bake to be busy," she said. Femi agreed at once.

A neighbor child wandered by looking pink in the cheeks and cheerful anyway. Nia still offered a spare hat from the basket. "Only if you want it," she said, because kindness was an offer, not a push. The child wanted it. Nia adjusted the brim and pointed to the shady bench. "There is room." Soon the bench held two hats, a cool can, and a little pause. Femi looked at Nia. "You brought more than cloth. You brought a way to share the garden's shadow." Nia felt pleased in a quiet way. She had not needed a grand speech. She had needed eyes and a basket.

They returned to the row with a new rhythm. One plant in the sun's full light, then a step back into shade for a breath and a sip. Nia kept the extra hat ready in case the borrowed one had to go home. She also turned a small board into a standing shade for the newest seedlings, copying an idea Femi liked. "Is this helpful or in the way?" she asked. Femi checked the angle. "Helpful. The leaves still see the sky, and the soil gets a break." Asking made the kindness accurate. Accurate kindness was the kind that fit.

When the watering was done, Nia poured the last cool water at the roots, slowly, the way Femi showed her. She did not splash for show. She watched the soil drink. Femi watched her and said, "You are gentle with them." Nia shrugged happily. "You needed a hat. They needed a slow drink. Same idea, different sizes." They both laughed. The clear sky felt like gentle weather they could greet with brims and branches and good timing. The garden smelled like wet leaves and warm straw.

Before leaving, Nia walked Femi to his own hat and waited while he put it on so the borrowed one could rest. She did not say he had been careless. There was no need. He already said, "I will park my hat in the shade next time, so it is easy to remember." Nia nodded. "And I will keep a spare, so a forgetful minute does not become an uncomfortable hour." That promise was kind to the future, not only to the afternoon. They bumped hat brims in a hello and a goodbye at once.

The path home was bright, so Nia wore a hat herself and carried the basket on the shady side of the lane. She liked how a small thing could change a face from a squint to a smile. The plants would be all right. Femi would be all right. The day could be sunny and still be soft around the edges if someone noticed.

Nia and Femi took one slow lap along the shady side of the garden before she left. They named the cool places out loud so the map would stick. Bench. Branch. Side of the shed. Hook for the can. Femi said he would tell Stig about the lap, because a kind route should be shared with the next worker too. Nia liked that her basket of hats had started a bigger kindness than a single squint. The plants looked settled. The bright sky looked friendly again. They parted at the gate with a promise to save the green hat for anyone whose eyes asked for shade.

The lantern by the window glowed softly next to the basket of sun hats. Nia smoothed the green brim and thought of the garden bench. Outside, the evening sky was calm and clear. She felt glad that kindness had been as practical as shade, and as easy to pass along as a hat held out at the right moment.
